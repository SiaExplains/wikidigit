Sequoia Capital said on November 4 that it is closing its London office, has ended its Arc accelerator and Scouts program, and has merged its early-stage and growth teams, while also reporting about $10 billion in new funds. The firm's seven general partners signed the changes in [an X article called "The Sequoia of Tomorrow"](https://x.com/sequoia/status/2117961312143855764), which said Sequoia is "coming out of a ~20 year expansionary phase." The article had passed 990,000 views by Thursday morning. [TechCrunch reads it](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/sequoia-hits-rewind-undoing-20-years-of-expansion/) as a shift to a smaller firm centered on the Bay Area.

## What Sequoia Is Shutting Down

Arc launched in March 2022, and TechCrunch describes it as Sequoia's answer to Y Combinator: a batch accelerator for pre-seed and seed founders, with several seven-week cohorts a year and $500,000 to $1 million up front for admitted companies.

Scouts is much older. Sequoia started it around 2009, giving small checks to founders and operators to invest on its behalf. According to TechCrunch, Jason Calacanis pointed Sequoia to Uber through the program, and Sam Altman brought in Stripe as a scout. Other early scouts included Brian Chesky, plus Arash Ferdowsi and Drew Houston of Dropbox.

The London office opened in 2020. Sequoia said closing it means "several people will be leaving," and added, "This is not about the team - they are excellent." "We simply don't see the need for a local presence," it wrote. The firm said it still expects Europe to produce "outlier founders and companies" and that it can support them from the Bay Area.

Arc and Scouts appear in the memo under the heading "Focus." "We are best at high-conviction, highly selective venture capital investing," it says. It also mentions removing "much of the complexity associated with the Sequoia Capital Fund," without explaining what that means.

## One Team, Seven Partners, No Monday Meeting

"There is no more 'Early & Growth'. There is only Sequoia," the memo says, arguing that a single team makes things smoother as companies move from stage to stage.

Sequoia also says its expansion had an unintended side effect, hierarchy. Running multiple businesses in multiple regions "required real management," the memo says, and that "crept over into the investing team." So the partnership has been flattened, and the Monday partner meeting cycle is gone. "Founders shouldn't have to adhere to our process," it says.

Seven general partners now own and operate the firm: co-stewards Alfred Lin and Pat Grady, plus Andrew Reed, Shaun Maguire, Konstantine Buhler, Sonya Huang and David Cahn. Former Workday CEO Carl Eschenbach returned as a full-time partner in March, per TechCrunch. The memo came a year to the day after [Sequoia named Lin and Grady to replace Roelof Botha](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/04/sequoia-names-alfred-lin-and-pat-grady-as-new-co-stewards-as-roelof-botha-steps-down/) as its top leaders. TechCrunch says Botha was voted out and cut ties with the firm this summer, around the time he joined SpaceX's board.

On the Monday meeting, TechCrunch notes that [Benchmark's partners](/article/benchmark-vc-no-rivals-rule-factory-cognition) suggested at Disrupt last month that they now mostly use theirs to socialize.

## The Partner Who Said No to San Francisco

Luciana Lixandru, whom Sequoia hired from Accel in 2020 as its first European partner, [wrote on X](https://x.com/LucianaLix) that she was offered a move to San Francisco and turned it down. "Europe is where my family is, and where I want to build," she wrote.

She said she will remain a partner and keep her board seats while investing personally in European founders, with more on her next steps to come. She credited her London colleagues for early work with companies including Pennylane, Trade Republic and Aspora.

Other senior roles have changed too. TechCrunch reports that Stephanie Zhan, who joined in 2015, has left and said last month she is starting her own firm. Jess Lee, the firm's first female partner, who oversaw Arc, is now listed as chief product officer and no longer appears on the investor page.

[Silicon Republic notes](https://siliconrepublic.com/business/sequoia-closes-london-office-sunsets-arc-and-scouts-initiatives) that Andreessen Horowitz closed its office there early last year and Coatue shut its European venture office in 2024, each about two years after opening. In 2023, Sequoia also separated from its China and India/Southeast Asia arms, which became HongShan and Peak XV.

## What the $10 Billion Doesn't Tell You

The fundraising gets one sentence in the memo: "We've raised ~$10Bn in new funds." It doesn't name the funds or split the money by stage, and TechCrunch attributes the number to Sequoia. Silicon Republic reports that the money closed earlier in 2026. The memo doesn't date the Arc and Scouts decisions, so it doesn't show which came first.

Sequoia is still writing into big rounds. It co-led [Kalshi's $1 billion round at a $40 billion valuation](/article/kalshi-40-billion-utah-felony-charges) last week and returned as a backer in [Flapping Airplanes' $1 billion Series A](/article/flapping-airplanes-1-billion-6-billion-no-product) in October. [Crunchbase counts $59 billion](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/october-2026-global-startup-funding-strong-china-deepseek/) in global venture funding for October, with just over 40% going to seven companies and 66% to AI-driven ones.

For founders, two early routes into Sequoia are gone, a batch program and a network of scouts with small checks, and the memo doesn't say what replaces them. European founders will deal with a firm supporting them from the Bay Area. Sequoia's stated goal is "not scale, but quality," which it defines as the best possible returns to its investors and being the best possible partner to founders. The memo also concedes that "it is scary to break something that's already working just fine."
