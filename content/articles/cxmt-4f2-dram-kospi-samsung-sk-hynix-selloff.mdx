China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) said over the weekend that it will ship server memory built on a denser "4F²" DRAM design before the end of 2026, roughly two years ahead of Samsung's own timeline, and the claim alone was enough to knock South Korea's Kospi down [5.46% to 6,035.35 on October 13](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/122131), its steepest drop since August. CXMT has not published a single specification, yield figure or volume target. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell for a second day, Micron and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index slid in New York, and foreign investors dumped 4.53 trillion won of Korean stocks. The twist is that most analysts don't think CXMT is about to overtake anyone. The market sold anyway, which says as much about how stretched the AI memory trade had become as it does about China.

## What CXMT Actually Said

CXMT President Cao Kanyu made the claim at the 4th Integrated Circuit and Chiplet Conference in Shanghai on October 9 and 10, [according to Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/10/13/chinese-memory-makers-tech-claim-sinks-samsung-sk-hynix). The company plans to release DDR5 RDIMM modules for enterprise servers using a 4F² cell layout by year-end, built on what it calls a self-developed Vertical Surrounding Gate transistor. Until now CXMT has focused on LPDDR memory for phones and laptops. A company spokesperson [confirmed Cao's comments to reporters](https://gulfnews.com/technology/china-chip-claim-rattles-samsung-and-sk-hynix-what-is-the-4f-breakthrough-1.500708938) but added nothing more.

That is the whole announcement. [CXMT did not disclose technical specifications or a specific launch date](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/samsung-sk-hynix-shares-drop-as-chinas-cxmt-flags-progress-in-memory-development-4943096), and Seoul Economic Daily notes it has not described its process, implementation method or yields either.

## Why 4F² Spooked the Memory Giants

Today's mainstream DRAM uses a 6F² cell, where F is the smallest line width on the chip. Shrinking the cell to 4F² cuts its area by about a third and could raise density by roughly 30% to 50% on the same silicon. The catch is that it needs an upright transistor with a vertical channel and a gate wrapped around it, a structure that has fought leakage and manufacturing complexity for years.

The Korean leaders are working on the same thing. SK Hynix published a 4F² roadmap in 2025, and Samsung is targeting mass production of sub-10-nanometre DRAM with the design in 2028. If CXMT ships first, it would be roughly two years ahead, and it would do it while U.S. sanctions block it from buying ASML's EUV lithography machines. The industry reads 4F² as a way around that ban, because the vertical structure can be built with older DUV tools.

It also lands on a company that is already growing fast. Counterpoint Research data shows CXMT's share of global DRAM reached 10% in the second quarter of 2026, up from 4% a year earlier, and a summer IPO briefly made it China's most valuable company. It is the same self-reliance push that saw Beijing [turn away NVIDIA's US-approved H200 chips](/article/nvidia-h200-china-refuses-us-approved-chips).

## Two Days, 16% and 22%

The selloff came in two waves. On Monday, October 12, Samsung fell 3.6% to 252,500 won and SK Hynix 3.4% to 1,624,000 won, while CXMT's own shares dropped 3.1% to 49.42 yuan. In New York that night, Micron fell 3.88%, Intel 3.20% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 2.67%, according to the Korea Economic Daily.

Tuesday was worse. The Kospi broke below 6,000 intraday for the first time since July 31, triggering a sell-side sidecar for the first time in two months. Samsung closed at 234,000 won and SK Hynix at 1,468,000 won; outlets put the day's declines at around 7% for Samsung and between 8.5% and 10% for SK Hynix depending on the cut-off. Over five sessions, Samsung lost 16% and SK Hynix 22%. Both had topped 2,000 trillion won in market value during this year's boom and ended the day in the roughly 1,000 trillion won range. Foreign investors net sold 4.5259 trillion won, their sixth straight session of selling, while retail investors bought 2.2 trillion won. Korea's volatility index jumped 19% to 48.53.

That is a sharp reversal for the companies that pledged [4,755 trillion won to keep South Korea's chip lead](/article/south-korea-samsung-sk-4755-trillion-won-chip-ai-plan) only in June. The Kospi now sits 36% below its record intraday high of 9,385.59, set on June 19.

## The Selloff Was Looking for a Reason

Analysts were cautious about the claim itself. Morningstar's Jing Zhewei said CXMT's position would improve significantly if it applies 4F² quickly and successfully, but expects a meaningful gap between unveiling a product and making it at profitable yields. CXMT is also still a latecomer in high-bandwidth memory, the stacked DRAM that sits next to NVIDIA's AI accelerators and drives much of Samsung's and SK Hynix's profit.

Others pointed at positioning. Bei Fu of Union Bancaire Privée told Bloomberg that investors seem nervous and are looking for a reason to take profits in AI stocks, especially memory names that have outperformed this year. Daishin Securities' Lee Kyung-min said the cushion from corporate buybacks has disappeared, and Eugene Investment & Securities' Huh Jae-hwan said the real question is "whether domestic demand for stocks is strong enough to offset" foreign selling.

The memory boom has been strong enough to [push Apple into a $100 iPhone price rise](/article/apple-iphone-18-pro-orders-cut-15-percent). This week showed how quickly that confidence turns when a rival says it can make the same product more cheaply, even before it shows anyone the product. By Friday the selling had spread far beyond memory, as [AI debt fears handed the Nasdaq its worst week since June](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week).
