*Correction (October 9, 2026): An earlier version of this article described xAI as SpaceX's "sister company"; SpaceX acquired xAI earlier in 2026, so xAI and Grok are part of SpaceX. It also listed Cursor as a compute tenant with a "$10 billion collaboration deal." In April, SpaceX secured the right to either buy Cursor for $60 billion or pay $10 billion for their work together, and on June 16 it agreed to buy Cursor outright. We also corrected the combined monthly value of the Anthropic and Google contracts (about $2.17 billion, not more than $2.3 billion), removed OpenAI from the list of companies renting SpaceX compute, added Oracle to the Pentagon list, and removed Reflection's headcount and model-release status, which we could not verify as of publication.*

On June 22, an open-source AI startup called Reflection [signed a deal to pay SpaceX $150 million a month](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Reflection-AI-closes-billion-dollar-deal-with-SpaceX-for-compute-capacity-11341275.html) for access to its Colossus data center complex in Memphis, Tennessee. The deal is worth up to $6.3 billion if it runs through 2029. Reflection is not a household name — it was [founded in March 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/09/reflection-raises-2b-to-be-americas-open-frontier-ai-lab-challenging-deepseek) and had about 60 staff when it raised $2 billion last October. But on May 1 it was one of the companies the Pentagon [cleared to deploy AI on its classified networks](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/05/pentagon-clears-7-tech-firms-to-deploy-their-ai-on-its-classified-networks/), alongside OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Nvidia, SpaceX and Oracle — by far the youngest company on the list. The fact that it chose SpaceX for compute is not incidental. It is the latest data point in a story nobody has cleanly named yet: SpaceX is now the compute landlord for American AI.

## The Colossus Tenant List

The Reflection deal lands Musk's company in a position that would have seemed implausible two years ago. Anthropic is using [all of the compute capacity at Colossus 1](https://www.anthropic.com/news/higher-limits-spacex) — more than 300 megawatts and over 220,000 Nvidia GPUs — and, according to SpaceX's IPO filing, is [paying $1.25 billion a month through May 2029](https://www.techloy.com/spacex-ipo-filing-reveals-anthropic-pays-41-million-a-day/). Google is [paying $920 million a month starting in October](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-spacex-920-million-month-compute-deal), for access to approximately 110,000 GPUs through June 2029. And now Reflection adds $150 million a month starting July 1, drawing on [Nvidia's GB300 systems at Colossus 2](https://thenextweb.com/news/spacex-reflection-ai-6-3bn-compute-deal). The Anthropic and Google contracts alone add up to about $2.17 billion a month from two customers. Cursor, the AI coding company, is a different case: SpaceX [agreed on June 16 to buy it outright](https://thenextweb.com/news/spacex-cursor-acquisition-official-60-billion) in a $60 billion all-stock deal, so it is becoming part of the landlord rather than a tenant. The company has turned a data center built for xAI's Grok models into one of the most lucrative infrastructure platforms in the AI industry, without launching a traditional cloud product, without building a marketplace, and without any of the usual enterprise software apparatus that AWS or Azure requires.

## The Open-Source Angle

Reflection's inclusion in this roster is worth examining on its own terms. The company is building open-weight frontier models — it has said its first would be trained on "tens of trillions of tokens" — and plans to release the weights publicly. Its founders are Misha Laskin, who led reward modeling for DeepMind's Gemini project, and Ioannis Antonoglou, one of the co-creators of AlphaGo. The team is small, technically elite, and deliberately building something that governments can use without depending on the closed ecosystems of OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google. The Pentagon's decision to include Reflection in its classified-network agreements — covering Impact Level 6 and 7, the environments for secret and more highly classified systems — suggests the government wants an open-weight option alongside the major labs. The strategic logic is straightforward: if some government systems can't depend on commercial APIs, you need models with open weights that can be deployed on sovereign infrastructure. Reflection is building for exactly that use case.

## The Landlord Paradox

Here is the tension at the center of this story: through xAI, which it acquired earlier this year, SpaceX is itself in direct competition with Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI on frontier model development. Grok competes with Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT. Yet SpaceX is renting compute to two of those rivals — and pocketing billions in the process. The arrangement creates a peculiar dynamic. Musk's infrastructure business profits regardless of which AI company wins the model race. If Anthropic trains a better model on Colossus hardware, SpaceX gets paid. If Google uses Colossus to push Gemini forward, SpaceX gets paid. If Reflection releases the open-weight model that disrupts all of the above, SpaceX gets paid. This is a position closer to landlord than competitor — and it may prove more durable than any single model advantage. Models become obsolete. Compute infrastructure, especially infrastructure with the GPU density and power capacity that Colossus has built, keeps finding tenants.

## What the Tenant List Reveals

The composition of SpaceX's compute customers is a map of where American AI is actually being built. The list does not include Microsoft, which has its own data center infrastructure tied to OpenAI. It does not include Meta, which is building out its own massive GPU clusters. What it does include is the set of companies — Anthropic, Google, Reflection — that either needed capacity faster than they could build it, or needed to scale compute without the capital expenditure of owning the hardware outright. SpaceX offered both: immediate access to high-density GPU infrastructure and contracts that, after initial periods, let either side walk away with 90 days' notice. That optionality has a price — $150 million a month for Reflection, $1.25 billion a month for Anthropic — but it is faster than building and available now. The result is that Elon Musk, whose public persona in AI centers almost entirely on Grok, is quietly collecting rent from the companies building the models that compete with his. The scale of that rent — measured in billions per month across the tenant list — may end up being the more historically significant part of the SpaceX AI story. Infrastructure is the pattern beyond AI too: SpaceX is now [buying the 800 MHz airwaves T-Mobile just sold](/article/spacex-8-billion-800mhz-spectrum-starlink-mobile) to turn Starlink into a mobile carrier.
