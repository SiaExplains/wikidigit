Apple is shutting down the Apple Support Community, the user-run help forum it has hosted in one form or another since 1998. The site stopped taking new posts on November 10, 2026, and a notice at the top of [discussions.apple.com](https://discussions.apple.com/) says it "will permanently close on November 20, 2026." Apple told [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/10/apple-support-community-closing-november-20/) the decision came "after a careful evaluation of strategy and customer needs," and offered no other reason. Apple also hasn't said whether the archive, years of threads where customers fixed each other's Macs and iPhones, will still be online after the 20th.

## Read-Only Since November 10

Apple doesn't appear to have announced the change ahead of time. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/10/end-of-an-era-apple-support-community-forums-will-permanently-close-on-november-20/) noticed that the newest post on the site was less than an hour old when it wrote up the closure, which suggests posting was turned off that same evening.

The notice itself is short: "Starting today, the site will no longer accept new posts and is available in a read-only state." It sends people to the official [Apple Support site](https://support.apple.com) instead. Apple also thanked the community members who "have contributed their time and expertise to help others with their Apple products."

For the next week or so, the old content still works. Visitors can browse discussions, search them, and press the "Me too" and voting buttons. They can't ask a new question or reply to one. The "Sign in" button now goes to an AppleConnect login page labelled "AC Communities Web – Internal," which 9to5Mac read as a sign that only Apple employees can log in now. MacRumors described the same page as an internal portal for Apple corporate staff. iClarified noted that the closure banner shows up on the homepage and on individual community pages, and that user-written tips are still readable next to the old discussions.

The version of the notice 9to5Mac saw called the site the "AppleCare Support Community," which isn't the forum's actual name.

## From Apple Tech Exchange to Read-Only

According to MacRumors, the forum started in 1998 as the Apple Tech Exchange and became Apple Discussions in 2000. In April 2011 Apple relaunched it as Apple Support Communities, with a new design, personal profiles and a reputation system that rewarded helpful users. [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/103440/apple-support-community-forums-to-permanently-close-on-november-20) notes a 2014 redesign that added instant search and organized the site by product. The last rename came in 2022, when it became the singular Apple Support Community. MacRumors puts the site's age, counting every version, at nearly 30 years.

## The Question Nobody Has Answered: What Happens to the Archive

As iClarified pointed out, the notice doesn't say whether existing discussions stay up after November 20, and Apple's statement to MacRumors doesn't mention the archive either. 9to5Mac asked Apple for comment about the scope of the closure and hadn't heard back when it published.

Search for an obscure Apple error and a Support Community thread is often near the top of the results, so this is the bit most people will notice. Anyone who depends on a particular thread has about a week to save a copy. If Apple does take the pages down, every blog post, help-desk article and forum reply that links to them will point at nothing.

The simplest way to keep a thread is to open it in a browser and save it as a PDF from the print dialog. Submitting the page to a web archive service also works, as long as it happens before the 20th.

Not everyone will miss the place. The top comment under 9to5Mac's story, from a reader named Josh Rocker, said the forum ranked well in search but that its threads often went nowhere or ended in a link to an unhelpful Apple support page.

## Where Apple Users Go Next

Apple's own answer is its official support site, plus [getsupport.apple.com](https://getsupport.apple.com) for contacting the company directly. MacRumors pointed readers to its own forums and to the r/applehelp subreddit.

The closure lands during a busy stretch for Apple's product line, which is dealing with [a backordered foldable iPhone and reports of cracked screens](/article/apple-iphone-duo-backordered-screen-cracks) and [reported cuts to iPhone 18 Pro orders](/article/apple-iphone-18-pro-orders-cut-15-percent). Owners with problems like those used to post in the Support Community. From November 20 they'll have Apple's support pages, AppleCare and third-party forums instead. Apple's [ChatGPT hand-off inside Siri](/article/openai-vs-apple-chatgpt-siri-partnership) has had troubles of its own, and Apple hasn't linked it to the forum's closure.
