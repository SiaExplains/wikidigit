The U.S. Treasury moved on Friday, November 6, to cut crypto exchange MEXC Global off from the American financial system. FinCEN, Treasury's financial crimes unit, named MEXC a "primary money laundering concern" and alleges it handled at least $16 billion in illicit activity tied to Russia, Iran and North Korea between May 2021 and May 2026. That total includes about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges, at a company that publicly says it bars users in Iran and North Korea.

## What the Order Actually Does

The order uses Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act rather than a standard sanctions designation. That provision lets Treasury act against foreign financial institutions tied to Russian illicit finance. Once the order is in force, U.S. financial institutions can't send funds to MEXC or receive funds from it, and CoinDesk reports that this includes crypto transfers. It takes effect 30 days after it appears in the Federal Register.

Treasury has used Section 9714 on crypto exchanges twice before, against Bitzlato in 2023 and PM2BTC in 2024. CoinDesk describes both as far smaller than MEXC, which makes this the largest exchange hit under the provision so far. Treasury says MEXC processes more than $1 billion in trades a day and holds about $5 billion in reserves. Founded in 2018, it lists more than 3,000 tokens, per Decrypt, and ranked among the world's 20 largest crypto exchanges as of September.

## The $16 Billion, Broken Down

The headline figure covers five years and several kinds of activity. According to FinCEN's findings as reported by CoinDesk and Decrypt, nearly $1.5 billion is linked to Russian sanctions evasion. That includes business with Garantex, which the U.S. sanctioned more than four years ago, and its successor Grinex; Decrypt reports MEXC was still dealing with both as recently as June 2026.

On Iran, FinCEN counts about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges, plus roughly $22 million that benefited Iran's central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Decrypt adds $232 million in direct transactions with sanctioned entities and about $200 million linked to the A7A5 stablecoin.

North Korea is part of the $16 billion total, but none of the coverage breaks out a separate figure for it. FinCEN also said MEXC's anti-money laundering controls had "severe deficiencies," which is hard to square with the exchange's public policy of keeping out users from Iran and North Korea.

## A Russia Law Aimed at Iran

The statute is about Russia, but Treasury is presenting this as an Iran action. It falls under Operation Economic Outcast, the campaign Treasury launched in August to cut Iran off from global finance. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said "financial institutions cannot have it both ways" and promised to keep severing the Iranian regime's "economic lifelines."

MEXC is the latest in a run of crypto targets this year. The Block notes that Treasury sanctioned Nobitex, Iran's largest crypto exchange, in June, saying it processed more than half of Iran's crypto inflows in 2025. In September it sanctioned BitBank, another Iranian exchange, over alleged bitcoin transfers to the IRGC. Last month it designated Russia's A7 network a transnational criminal organization, proposed cutting its front companies off from U.S. finance and flagged more than $179 billion in transactions through the A7A5 token, Decrypt reports.

MEXC had already started pulling back from Russia. CoinDesk reports the exchange announced last month that it would stop serving users in Russia and Belarus. FinCEN's Russia figures run through May 2026, and the Garantex and Grinex dealings through June, so that announcement came after the period the order describes.

## What MEXC Says

MEXC said it is reviewing the order, continues to operate normally and has started an independent review of its anti-money laundering and sanctions controls, according to CoinDesk. The Block reports the company says it maintains AML programs and blocks users in countries including Iran and North Korea.

None of this applies yet. The 30-day clock starts only when the order is published in the Federal Register, and after that the restriction falls on U.S. banks, payment firms and other financial institutions, which will have to stop moving money to and from the exchange.

The timing is odd for an industry that has spent the year getting closer to the regulated system. Two weeks ago Ripple won [a federal trust bank charter from the OCC](/article/ripple-national-trust-bank-occ-charter-icba-lawsuit), and Kalshi [raised $1 billion at a $40 billion valuation](/article/kalshi-40-billion-utah-felony-charges) a day after Utah filed felony charges against it. Washington is letting some crypto firms in and shutting others out, much as it has used access to American chips as leverage in [its export fight with China](/article/nvidia-h200-china-refuses-us-approved-chips).
