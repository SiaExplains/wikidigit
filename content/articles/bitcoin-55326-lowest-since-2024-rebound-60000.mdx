Bitcoin fell to $55,326 early on Saturday, November 21, its lowest price since September 2024, and the slide wiped out about $2.4 billion in leveraged crypto bets in 24 hours, according to Investing.com and CoinGlass data. By 09:50 ET the same morning it had climbed back to about $60,312, which still left it more than half below its October 2025 record.

## How Far It Fell, and How Fast

The low came in the early hours of Saturday. Data compiled by 24/7 Wall St. shows the 03:30 UTC fifteen-minute bar falling from $58,156.74 to $56,471, and the next bar reaching $55,500. Exact lows differ by data source: Investing.com puts it at $55,326, 24/7 Wall St. at $55,106 and CoinGlass at about $55,127, per crypto.news.

Saturday's move also broke through about $57,800, the bear-market low set on July 1. On Friday, bitcoin had already closed at $58,817.93, its lowest daily close in 90 days, after Crypto Briefing reported an intraday low of $57,716 on November 20 that erased every gain the asset had made since Donald Trump's re-election.

Bitcoin opened November at $77,558, so the month is down about 22%. The quarter is down roughly 27.5%. Against the record of about $126,000 set in October 2025, the price at mid-morning on Saturday was 52.3% lower, by 24/7 Wall St.'s math. Ether fell too, down about 3.2% to $1,687.16.

## $2.4 Billion in Forced Selling

Leverage made the drop sharper. When the price falls fast, exchanges automatically close out traders who borrowed to bet on a rise, and those forced sales push the price down further.

CoinGlass counted about $2.4 billion in crypto derivatives liquidations over 24 hours, Investing.com reported, including more than $1 billion in bitcoin futures and $545 million in ether positions. The largest single wipeout was a $51.71 million bullish bitcoin position. The Kobeissi Letter put the total higher, at $2.7 billion, and wrote on X: "This officially marks the 11th-largest liquidation event in crypto market history." For scale, crypto.news notes that is about 14% of the roughly $19.3 billion liquidated on October 10, 2025.

About $650 million was liquidated in a single hour, per crypto.news. On Kraken, the BTC/CAD pair briefly printed C$24,250. Kraken Support said "Executed trades stand" and reported no security breach.

## The ETF Money Was Already Leaving

Money had been leaving before Saturday. US spot bitcoin ETFs had net outflows on three straight days: $239.94 million on November 17, $306.42 million on November 18 and $582.19 million on November 19, according to 24/7 Wall St. Farside Investors data cited by crypto.news shows $541.1 million of that last day came out of BlackRock's IBIT, and puts November's outflows at about $3.47 billion through that session. Total assets in the funds dropped from $104.88 billion on October 28 to $76.55 billion on November 19.

What pushed sellers over the edge is less clear. Investing.com tied it to a broader risk-off day: the Nasdaq fell more than 4% on Friday to its weakest level since April, amid worries about AI valuations [after Anthropic's record IPO](/article/anthropic-ipo-79-billion-record-nasdaq-falls), and Anthropic's shares fell more than 14%, erasing their gains over the $83 offering price. 24/7 Wall St. was more cautious, writing that "No single trigger from outside crypto has been confirmed." The worry about AI spending that hit [CoreWeave's stock and the Nasdaq earlier this month](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week) has now reached crypto.

Investing.com also noted that Fed Governor Christopher Waller raised the possibility of a 50-basis-point rate increase, and Delphi Digital's Scott Freeman warned that declining liquidity could amplify losses.

## Bounce or Bottom?

At 14:39 UTC bitcoin was at $60,135, and during the recovery CoinGlass recorded $68.82 million in short liquidations against $1.64 million in longs within an hour, per crypto.news. Traders who had bet on further falls were being forced to buy back in.

Analysts are split on what it means. Trader Michaël van de Poppe said his Z-score reading hit -3, which he said has happened only 10 times in 9.5 years, and wrote: "It doesn't signal a bottom. It does signal a bounce." He named $72,000 as a possible target. CryptoQuant's Amr Taha counted 92,100 BTC, about $5.34 billion, moved to exchanges at a loss by short-term holders on November 21, which he called consistent with "potential capitulation" but not a confirmed bottom. Lookonchain flagged Abraxas Capital withdrawing 17,597 BTC, about $1.04 billion, from Binance and Kraken at an average of $59,206.

Wintermute set a harder bar. It said bitcoin needs to reclaim $68,000, along with fresh inflows into spot ETFs, before a recovery looks convincing, and had warned earlier that recent rebounds were mostly short covering. On that test, Saturday's jump back above $60,000 doesn't qualify yet: the ETFs were still losing money as of the last reported session, and bitcoin is still worth less than half its October 2025 record.
