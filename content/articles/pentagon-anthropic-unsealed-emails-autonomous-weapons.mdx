*Correction (October 9, 2026): An earlier version of this article got the sequence of the emails wrong. The "just not workable" email came in January 2026, not the day before the blacklisting; the supply-chain risk designation was announced the day after Amodei's own email saying the Pentagon's language would remove Anthropic's red lines; and Emil Michael's "very close" email came after the designation was finalized, not before. Negotiations did not run "into September." We also removed an unverified reference to the GenAI.mil platform, an unsupported claim about how other AI companies have negotiated, and corrected the description of the court rulings, including a misquoted line from the D.C. Circuit.*

This week, court documents in Anthropic's lawsuit against the Defense Department in the Northern District of California were made public, [first reported by the Wall Street Journal and published in full by Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/read-the-tense-emails-between-the-pentagon-former-uber-exec-and-anthropic-dario-amodei-2000780849). They answer a question people have been asking since February: what actually broke down between Anthropic and the Pentagon before the Department of Defense labeled the company a "supply-chain risk to national security"? Part of the answer, in Emil Michael's own words, is that Anthropic's guardrails were "just not workable." Michael is the Pentagon's Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering, and the recipient of his email was Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, during negotiations over how the military could use Claude. The line reads less like a negotiating position and more like a verdict.

## Two Rules, One Collision

In its dealings with the Pentagon, Anthropic held to two hard limits: no use of Claude in [fully autonomous weapons and no mass domestic surveillance of Americans](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-comments-secretary-war). The Pentagon wanted coverage for "all lawful uses." What the emails show is what happened when those limits collided with a working relationship — the Defense Department had [awarded Anthropic a contract worth up to $200 million in July 2025](https://anthropic.com/news/anthropic-and-the-department-of-defense-to-advance-responsible-ai-in-defense-operations). In January 2026, after weeks of silence, Michael wrote that he was "hoping that we are closer to engaging with your revised POV." He went on to call Anthropic's position "just not workable" and offered "one more chance to align on core principles" before the two sides would have to part ways. In late February, Amodei told Michael that the Pentagon's proposed contract language appeared to "completely remove our redlines."

## The Timing Is the Story

The emails matter less for what they say about ideology and more for what they establish about sequence. The day after Amodei's "redlines" email, [Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-comments-secretary-war) that his department would designate Anthropic a supply-chain risk — a label Hegseth said would bar defense contractors, suppliers, and partners from working with the company, with up to six months for the department to transition away from Claude. Anthropic later said the formal designation was [narrower than initially implied](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/05/tech/pentagon-anthropic-supply-chain-risk). Then, on March 4 — the day after the designation was formally finalized — Michael [emailed Amodei](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/20/new-court-filing-reveals-pentagon-told-anthropic-the-two-sides-were-nearly-aligned-a-week-after-trump-declared-the-relationship-kaput/): "I think we are very close here." Anthropic has maintained since it sued in March that the designation was retaliation dressed up as a security finding. The Pentagon has maintained it was a legitimate risk assessment. Emails showing the department's own point man calling the talks "very close" a day after his department had already blacklisted the company make Anthropic's version considerably easier to believe, regardless of what a judge ultimately rules. Judge Rita Lin has already called that timing ["exceedingly difficult to square"](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319713/20260704/pentagon-blacklisted-anthropic-over-autonomous-weapons-limits-emails-reveal-very-close-talks.htm) with the government's position.

## The Courts Are Already Split

This isn't a hypothetical fight — it's already produced conflicting rulings. On March 26, Judge Lin in the Northern District of California [granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/26/nx-s1-5762971/judge-temporarily-blocks-anthropic-ban) blocking the designation and the presidential directive ordering all federal agencies to stop using Anthropic's technology. In April, the D.C. Circuit [denied Anthropic's request to stay the designation](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/08/d-c-circuit-declines-to-stay-department-of-wars-supply-chain-risk-designation-of-claude/) in a separate case while litigation continues, weighing "a relatively contained risk of financial harm to a single private company" against judicial management of how the department "secures vital AI technology" during an active military conflict. The net effect: Anthropic is currently locked out of DOD contracts specifically, but can still work with the rest of the federal government while the underlying question — can a national-security customer punish a vendor for refusing to remove its own ethical constraints — works its way through the courts.

## Why This Outlasts One Contract

The Anthropic-Pentagon fight is being watched closely by every AI lab with defense ambitions, because it's the first real test of whether a company's safety commitments survive contact with its largest possible customer. Anthropic drew its lines publicly and specifically — no fully autonomous weapons, no domestic surveillance — which made it the test case almost by default. If the DOD can successfully use a supply-chain risk designation as leverage to override a vendor's stated ethical limits, that's the playbook for every future negotiation with every future AI vendor. If Anthropic wins, it sets a precedent that a company can hold a specific line against the Pentagon and survive the retaliation. Either outcome will shape how the next AI-defense contract gets negotiated — this time with the emails from the last one already public.
