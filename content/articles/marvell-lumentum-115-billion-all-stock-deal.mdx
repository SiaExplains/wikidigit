Marvell Technology agreed on Monday, November 2, to buy optical components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal that values Lumentum at [$115 billion including debt](https://investor.marvell.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1040/marvell-and-lumentum-to-combine-creating-a-category-leader-in-ai-connectivity), betting that AI data centers will move to optical connections. When Bloomberg reported the talks on October 28, [Marvell's stock fell about 13% the next morning](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/29/marvell-sinks-13-lumentum-jumps-6-on-reported-acquisition-talks-coherent-holds-flat-applied-optoelectronics-drops-3/). Marvell signed anyway, and since it pays in its own shares, that drop also cut what Lumentum holders are getting.

## The Terms

Lumentum stockholders will get a fixed 4.500 Marvell shares for every Lumentum share. Marvell puts that at about $1,132 per share, a premium of about 23.9% to the October 28 closing prices. That was the last trading day before speculation started. [Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/marvell-to-buy-lumentum-in-115-billion-deal-in-ai-data-center-bet-ce785fd8d181f52d) puts the equity value at $102.54 billion.

Marvell stockholders will own about 67% of the combined company on a fully diluted basis, and Lumentum stockholders about 33%. Matt Murphy stays chairman and CEO. Three Lumentum directors, CEO Michael Hurlston among them, will join Marvell's board, which has seven members now. Both boards approved the deal unanimously. It still needs both sets of stockholders and the regulators, and the companies expect to close in the first half of calendar 2028.

The companies say they can see more than $1 billion a year in run-rate cost synergies, and they expect the deal to add to Marvell's non-GAAP earnings per share in the first year after closing. Reuters calls it Marvell's largest acquisition since it bought Inphi for $10 billion in 2021.

## Why Marvell Wants Lasers

Marvell already sells electrical and optical interconnect chips, switching and custom silicon. Lumentum brings lasers, optical transceivers and optical circuit switches, plus its own compound semiconductor fabs, including indium phosphide. Together they claim an addressable market of $550 billion.

Murphy laid out the thesis in the release: "AI infrastructure is undergoing a fundamental architectural shift toward optical connectivity." Hurlston said "surging AI data traffic requires rapid hardware innovation" and promised "extreme co-design" with customers. The pitch is that one company can design the chip and the laser together and ship pre-validated optical systems faster than two suppliers working separately.

Lumentum's own numbers are growing fast. Its [fiscal first-quarter revenue was $1.26 billion](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/marvell-agrees-115-billion-allstock-deal-to-buy-lumentum-2774627), up from $533.8 million a year earlier, and adjusted gross margin rose to 53.2% from 39.4%. It guided second-quarter revenue to $1.575 billion to $1.625 billion, above the $1.48 billion consensus. Lumentum noted that the expected sequential jump of about $350 million is more than the whole company's quarterly revenue two years ago. Reuters adds that a proposed US ban on new Chinese optical transceivers could push even more demand to domestic suppliers.

## The Fixed Ratio Problem

The exchange ratio is fixed at 4.5 shares, so the deal is only worth what Marvell's stock is worth. The $1,132 figure comes from Marvell's price on October 28. By the morning of October 29, Marvell was trading around $220, according to 24/7 Wall St., and at that price 4.5 shares come to roughly $990. Lumentum rose about 6% to $965 the same morning, close to that value.

Reuters reported that both stocks had fallen sharply since the talks surfaced, amid wider worries about an AI bubble. That selloff has been building for weeks, from [CoreWeave's debt scare that gave the Nasdaq its worst week since June](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week) to [Alphabet's 11% drop on the same day Bloomberg broke the Marvell talks](/article/alphabet-google-cloud-103-percent-stock-falls). Axel Rudolph of IG Group told Reuters the combination "could give it a much stronger hand" as data centers demand faster links. He added that investors are right to ask whether Marvell is paying too much.

## How Marvell Got Here

Reuters notes that Marvell was little known three years ago. It is now worth more than $170 billion, mostly on custom AI chips and networking for companies like Amazon. Annual revenue went from $3 billion in fiscal 2021 to $8.2 billion in fiscal 2026. In August it signed a deal to supply chips alongside Google's AI processors that could bring up to $120 billion in sales through fiscal 2033, with Google getting the right to buy a $12.2 billion stake. In February it closed the $3.5 billion purchase of Celestial AI. At its October investor day it forecast fiscal 2031 sales of $70 billion to $90 billion, about $37.5 billion of it from interconnect.

Nvidia invested $2 billion each in Marvell and Lumentum earlier this year, and in June Jensen Huang called Marvell the next "trillion-dollar company," Reuters reports. Not every rumored chip deal gets signed: Qualcomm's [reported $10 billion interest in Tenstorrent](/article/qualcomm-tenstorrent-10-billion-deal-denied) never went anywhere. Marvell also used Monday's announcement to narrow its fiscal third-quarter revenue guidance to ±2% from ±5%, keeping the midpoint, and Lumentum reports again on November 5. Until the deal closes, what Lumentum holders receive will rise and fall with Marvell's share price.
