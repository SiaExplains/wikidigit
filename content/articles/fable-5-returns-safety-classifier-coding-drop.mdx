*Correction (October 9, 2026): An earlier version of this article described the 70% drop as a fall in Fable 5's own benchmark scores; it was measured by BridgeMind's third-party BridgeBench suite, which scores every rerouted task as zero. It also said rerouted requests were downgraded "silently" (Anthropic says users are notified when a fallback happens), described the Amazon finding as a "working exploit," misstated the July 7 usage terms, and repeated a "90 minutes' notice" claim the reporting does not support as written. Those passages have been corrected using the sources linked below.*

Claude Fable 5 came back online on July 1, 19 days after the US Commerce Department's export-control order forced Anthropic to pull it from global circulation. That much is a clean win: [the export controls were lifted on June 30](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5), and Fable 5 is once again available across the Claude Platform, Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork, with AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry to follow. What did not make it into the celebratory headlines is what Anthropic had to bolt onto the model to get the relaunch done — a safety classifier aggressive enough that, on one third-party benchmark, Fable 5's debugging score [fell by roughly 70%](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319576/20260702/claude-fable-5-debugging-scores-drop-70-safety-classifier-reroutes-tasks-weaker-fallback-model.htm) within a day of shipping.

## How We Got Here

The original shutdown traced back to a technique Amazon researchers discovered: prompting Fable 5 to identify software vulnerabilities, which in one case led the model to produce code demonstrating how a vulnerability could be exploited. Anthropic says every model it tested, including Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5, could produce the same demonstration. That finding, combined with a separate dispute over SK Telecom's admission into Anthropic's Glasswing security consortium, led the White House to lose confidence in Anthropic, and a [June 12 directive](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) barred foreign nationals from Fable 5 and its research sibling Mythos 5 — which, because Anthropic could not check nationality in real time, meant shutting both down worldwide. Anthropic spent nearly three weeks in a standoff over whether the jailbreak was even a real vulnerability or ordinary prompt sensitivity. The relaunch ended that argument by making the classifier stricter rather than settling who was right.

## A 99% Fix With a 70% Cost

The new classifier blocks the reported technique in more than 99% of cases, which is the number Anthropic is leading with. The number it isn't leading with: when BridgeMind re-ran its BridgeBench suite on the July 1 redeploy, [only 3 of 12 TypeScript debugging tasks actually reached Fable 5](https://decrypt.co/372750/claude-fable-5-not-nerfed-router-paranoid) — the other 9 were flagged, rerouted to the fallback model Opus 4.8, and scored zero, dropping the debugging score from 86.2 to 25.9. BridgeBench scores every fallback as zero because the model that answered isn't the one under evaluation, so the drop measures routing, not a dumber model. But that's still the majority of an ordinary coding session getting handed to a weaker model because the request pattern-matches against the same framing the Amazon researchers used. Anthropic's own explanation is candid about the tradeoff: the new classifier flags benign requests more often during routine coding and debugging, and its trigger zone is deliberately set to catch probably-benign requests along with genuinely harmful ones, because Anthropic chose to accept more false positives in exchange for fewer missed harmful requests.

## The Meter Starts July 7

The commercial mechanics compound the problem. Through July 7, Fable 5 is included for Pro, Max, Team, and select Enterprise plans for up to 50% of their weekly usage limits at no extra cost — a clear attempt to get developers back into the habit of reaching for the model before the credit meter starts running. After July 7, Fable 5 moves to paid usage credits for those same tiers. Anthropic says users are told when a request falls back to Opus 4.8, though at least one third-party coding tool has [reported that the fallback isn't visible](https://github.com/can1357/oh-my-pi/issues/4177) in its interface. Developers who spend the next four days testing whether the model is back to normal are, in a meaningful number of cases, going to find that their debugging request became an Opus 4.8 request — and then start paying credits for a model that, in practice, often doesn't handle the coding work it was known for.

## What "Restored" Actually Means

This is the part of the export-control saga that the "models are restored" headlines miss: the ban didn't just pause Fable 5, it changed how the model is served. The previous article on this fight ended with an open question — whether the models would return "exactly as they were," and what it would mean if they did. The answer is that they didn't. Anthropic absorbed the government's pressure by making the model measurably less useful for its most common professional use case, and shipped that as the cost of coming back online. Every other frontier lab now has a precedent to point to: when a government forces your hand on jailbreak resistance, the fastest fix is a blunter classifier, and the users footing the bill for that fix are the ones running ordinary debugging sessions, not the ones trying to extract exploits.
