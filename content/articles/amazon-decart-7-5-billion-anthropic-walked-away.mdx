Amazon said on November 23 that it has signed a deal to buy the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack and bring the Israeli startup's three co-founders and their engineers in-house. Amazon didn't give a price. [Bloomberg puts it at $7.5 billion, according to TechCrunch](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/amazon-snaps-decart-ai-optimization-171423520.html), which is $1.5 billion more than Anthropic was reported to be offering before it pulled out of its own Decart talks in September.

The announcement came as a [post on Amazon's news site](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-decart-ai-optimization). It names co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf and says the deal is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance. Amazon says it started working with Decart in 2025.

## What Amazon Is Actually Buying

Amazon's post describes a purchase of "the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS)," plus the founders and "their team of AI researchers and engineers." It doesn't say Amazon is buying Decart the company. It also doesn't say what happens to Decart's world models, including the Oasis 3 model TechCrunch mentions.

TechCrunch describes DOS as software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware. When [Decart raised $300 million in May](/article/decart-300m-world-models-nvidia-lock-in), the pitch was that DOS lets a team move its models between chip vendors instead of staying tied to Nvidia. Nvidia joined that round as an investor.

Now the software is going to one of those vendors. [Globes reports](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-amazon-confirms-decart-acquisition-1001564520) that Amazon is making the acquisition through Annapurna Labs, its chip development arm, and will fold the technology into its Trainium and Inferentia processors. Nafea Bshara, who heads engineering at Annapurna, is leading the deal for Amazon, according to Globes.

"Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," Peter DeSantis, the Amazon senior vice president who oversees custom silicon, said in the announcement.

TechCrunch reports that Amazon has so far used Trainium chips in its own data centers and is preparing to sell them to customers as a competitor to Nvidia. Decart's technology, it adds, could make it easier for customers to run their workloads on Amazon chips. Amazon's post doesn't say whether DOS will keep supporting other vendors' chips.

## Anthropic, Then Nvidia, Then Amazon

The sale took most of the autumn. Per [Calcalist's account, published by Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/s1adklfyme), the process became public on August 10, when Calcalist reported that Decart was exploring a sale. Days later, Bloomberg reported that Anthropic was in talks to buy the company for about $6 billion.

Calcalist says the two sides exchanged draft agreements and that Anthropic had effectively finished its due diligence. The talks then collapsed "for several reasons," which Calcalist doesn't list, and Anthropic withdrew in early September. Anthropic has since gone public, [raising a record $79 billion in its IPO](/article/anthropic-ipo-79-billion-record-nasdaq-falls) on November 20.

Calcalist also reports that Nvidia had expressed interest before the Anthropic talks, but the founders preferred Anthropic's proposal. After Anthropic left, Decart went back to Nvidia, and Nvidia declined to reopen negotiations.

Amazon was the next potential buyer. [PYMNTS notes](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/amazon-nears-7-5-billion-purchase-of-ai-startup-decart/) that The Wall Street Journal reported Amazon's interest in October, and the Financial Times reported on November 21 that talks were at an advanced stage at roughly $7.5 billion. Calcalist says the agreement was signed on Sunday night. The FT also reported that Decart was an early adopter of Amazon's Trainium3 chip, which debuted late last year.

## The Price Comes With Five-Year Strings

At $7.5 billion, the deal would be worth almost twice the roughly $4 billion valuation of Decart's May round. Not all of that is paid at signing, and the reports differ on the mechanics.

Calcalist describes a combination of cash and Amazon shares, paid in several installments. Globes says the deal is structured in shares and is contingent on meeting specific targets over five years. Globes also reports that the three founders together hold about 64% of Decart and are set to share $4.8 billion, and that they have committed to staying at Amazon for five years. To receive the full amount, Globes says, they have to stay that long.

Neither Amazon nor Decart has confirmed any of those terms. TechCrunch says both companies did not immediately respond to its requests for comment.

## Another Chip Startup Chooses Between a Buyer and a Round

Decart isn't the only AI chip startup that drew takeover interest this year. [Tenstorrent held takeover talks with Intel and Qualcomm, then raised $1.5 billion in October and stayed independent](/article/tenstorrent-1-5-billion-series-e-keller-independent). Decart took the buyer, six months after raising $300 million.

What's still open is everything Amazon's post leaves out: the price, whether Decart continues as a company, and what becomes of its video and world-model work. The deal also still needs regulatory clearance, and Amazon hasn't said when it expects to close.
