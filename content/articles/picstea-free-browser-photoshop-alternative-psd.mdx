_Picstea's features and policies below come from its own website as of October 19, 2026. The privacy check was done by WikiDigit the same day._

Picstea is a new photo editor that runs entirely in your web browser and costs nothing. It has layers, masks, adjustment layers, smart objects, the Pen tool and recorded actions, and it opens and saves layered Photoshop (PSD) files. There is no account, no watermark and no limit on exports, and the company says your images are never uploaded. Adobe charges US$22.99 a month for Photoshop on an annual plan. Picstea's pitch is that for everyday photo editing, retouching and layered design, it is often all you need. The domain was registered on October 18, 2026, so this is a product in its first days.

## What Picstea Actually Does

Open [picstea.com](https://picstea.com) and you land in the editor. There is no landing page or sign-up wall in front of it. The layout will feel familiar to anyone who has used Photoshop: a menu bar with File, Edit, Image, Layer, Adjustments, Filter and Select, a tool strip on the left and panels on the right.

![Picstea's editor with its welcome panel open, listing private editing, editable layers, selection and painting tools, and PSD import and export](/images/articles/picstea-free-browser-photoshop-alternative-psd-site.png)
*Picstea's editor on October 19, 2026. Screenshot: WikiDigit.*

According to its documentation, the editor covers:

- **Layers:** pixel, shape, text, fill and adjustment layers, plus groups, 16 blend modes, masks and layer effects.
- **Selections:** marquee, lasso, magic wand and Color Range.
- **Painting and retouching:** brushes, gradients, Clone Stamp and Healing Brush, plus brushes that lighten, darken, saturate, blur, sharpen and smudge.
- **Vector work:** the Pen tool with editable paths, which can be turned into selections or vector masks.
- **Smart objects:** layers that scale without losing quality, with editable smart filters.
- **Automation:** recorded actions that can be replayed on one image or a whole batch.

Keyboard shortcuts follow Photoshop's conventions, such as V for Move and Ctrl/Cmd+J to duplicate a layer, so muscle memory carries over.

## PSD Files Open With Their Layers Intact

For many people, PSD support decides whether a Photoshop alternative is any use at all. Picstea's [PSD editor page](https://picstea.com/psd-editor) says it opens 8-bit RGB and greyscale PSDs up to 12,000 pixels per side. Text stays editable, and Levels, Curves, Hue/Saturation and other adjustment layers come through. So do shape and fill layers, embedded smart objects, and common layer styles such as drop shadow, stroke and bevel.

The limits are spelled out on the same page. Picstea can't open PSB (large document) files, 16- or 32-bit colour, or CMYK. Warped text, Photoshop's own smart filters and a few styles such as satin and inner glow don't carry over. When you save back to PSD, text, shapes, smart objects and styles keep their look but become pixels. Exports go to PSD, PNG, JPEG, WebP, SVG and PDF.

## Your Images Stay on Your Machine

Most free online editors process your file on a server. That is the same trade-off WikiDigit looked at when [Emojar rebuilt itself around browser-only tools](/article/emojar-pivot-264-free-browser-tools). Picstea says it does the opposite: images are "opened, edited, saved and exported in your browser," and projects live in the browser's storage on your device.

We checked what we could. When WikiDigit loaded the editor on October 19, every network request went to Picstea's own domain and no cookies were set. The site also sends a Content-Security-Policy header with `connect-src 'self'`, which tells the browser to block connections to any other server. That doesn't prove what happens on Picstea's own servers, but it rules out the common pattern of quietly shipping files to a third-party API. When [a single login can expose millions of people's records](/article/ets-california-student-data-breach), not holding the files at all is the simplest protection there is.

## How It Compares With Photoshop and Photopea

Picstea is unusually frank about where it falls short. Its [Photoshop alternative page](https://picstea.com/photoshop-alternative) describes its depth as "core editing and retouching" and Photoshop's as "far more features, including AI tools." It says Photoshop is still the right tool for CMYK print production, 16- and 32-bit or RAW images, AI generation, and 3D or video. Picstea's [FAQ](https://picstea.com/faq) confirms there is no generative fill, object selection or content-aware fill yet. It is also built for a mouse and keyboard, so it isn't really a phone editor.

What you get instead is the price and the setup. Adobe lists the Photoshop plan at [US$22.99 a month](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop/plans.html) on an annual commitment (US$263.88 prepaid), and the app has to be installed. Picstea works in current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.

The closest rival is Photopea, the long-running free browser editor that also opens PSDs. Photopea shows ads inside the editor and sells a [Premium account](https://www.photopea.com/api/accounts) to remove them. Picstea says it is funded by ads "on the website and blog, never inside the editor." The editor screen we loaded does reserve a sidebar slot labelled "Sponsored," which currently advertises Picstea's own ad space.

## Who Is Behind It

That part is unclear. Picstea's pages don't name a company or a founder, and the footer only says "© Picstea." Its blog already has 11 how-to guides, on layers and masks, Levels and Curves, the Pen tool, smart objects and batch actions, but no launch post or dates. Until the people behind it step forward, judge it on what you can test yourself: open a PSD, try the tools you use every week, and see whether you miss the subscription.
