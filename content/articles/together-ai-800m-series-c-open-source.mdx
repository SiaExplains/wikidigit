We also removed a claim that Together AI's bookings were "a fraction" of their current level a year ago, which we could not verify, and noted that the company's own blog post gives a narrower 6x-to-20x cost-savings range than the 6x-to-60x figure in its press release.*

Together AI [announced Wednesday](https://www.together.ai/blog/announcing-our-series-c) that it closed an $800 million Series C at an [$8.3 billion post-money valuation](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260701243402/en/Together-AI-Raises-%24800-Million-at-%248.3-Billion-Valuation-to-Make-Frontier-AI-Accessible-to-All) — more than double the [$3.3 billion valuation of its February 2025 Series B](https://justainews.com/companies/funding-news/together-ai-raises-800m-at-8-3b-valuation-in-series-c/). The round was led by Aramco Ventures, the venture arm of Saudi Arabia's state oil company, with NVIDIA, Vista Equity Partners, General Catalyst, and Emergence Capital also writing checks. That combination of investors, a state oil company's venture fund and the world's dominant chipmaker, is not a coincidence. It is a bet that the next phase of the AI buildout runs through infrastructure companies that make open-source models fast and cheap, not only through the handful of labs building closed ones.

Together AI's pitch has stayed consistent since it launched: instead of building a frontier model and charging for access to it, run the best open-source models — DeepSeek, Nemotron, MiniMax, Kimi — on optimized infrastructure and sell the inference. In its funding announcement, the company says customers have [reported cost savings of 6x to 60x](https://startupfortune.com/together-ai-raises-800-million-from-aramco-at-an-83-billion-valuation/) for equal or better performance compared with closed models (CEO Vipul Ved Prakash's blog post puts it at 6x to 20x). That is a serious claim, and the business results back it up in scale terms at least: the company says annual bookings crossed $1.15 billion last quarter.

## The Arms Race Has a Second Track

Every headline out of AI in 2026 has been about frontier labs raising enormous sums to train the next model — OpenAI's IPO filing, xAI's compute hunger, record private rounds. Together AI's raise is a reminder that a second, quieter arms race is happening underneath that one: the fight to own the infrastructure layer that makes AI cheap enough for a normal company to actually deploy. Enterprises don't need the absolute best model for most workloads. They need something good enough, running reliably, at a price that doesn't blow up their unit economics. That is the gap Together AI is built to fill, and $1.15 billion in annual bookings suggests the gap is real.

NVIDIA's participation is the more interesting signal. NVIDIA has invested in nearly every layer of the AI stack over the past two years, but backing Together AI specifically means backing a company whose entire business model depends on commoditizing the model layer while keeping compute demand high. If enterprises shift from closed frontier APIs to open-source models running on optimized inference stacks, GPU demand doesn't shrink — it possibly grows, because the total addressable market of "companies that can afford to run AI" expands. NVIDIA doesn't need OpenAI or Anthropic to win the model war. It needs someone to win the inference war, on any chip that happens to be sitting in a data center.

## Why a Saudi Oil Company Leads This Round

Aramco Ventures leading an $800 million round for an AI infrastructure company is its own story. Gulf capital has been circling AI infrastructure for two years, but leading a round of this size signals a shift from passive participation to active positioning. Saudi Arabia wants AI compute to be a pillar of its post-oil economy; owning a stake in a company that lets any enterprise run frontier-quality AI without needing an OpenAI-scale training budget fits that strategy directly. The round also came with [commitments for over 500 MW of compute capacity](https://www.together.ai/blog/announcing-our-series-c), to be capitalized independently by the new investors. It's a hedge against two futures at once — one where closed labs stay dominant, and one where open models eat their lunch — with the investor positioned to profit either way as long as inference demand keeps climbing.

## The Real Test Is Retention, Not the Round

Raising $800 million and hitting $1.15 billion in annual bookings is a strong headline, but the number that will actually determine whether Together AI is a durable business is retention once open-source model quality plateaus or closed labs cut their own prices. As compute costs fall, OpenAI and Anthropic can keep lowering API prices, narrowing the cost gap that Together AI's entire pitch depends on. If that gap closes, the "6x to 60x cheaper" argument gets a lot less compelling, and infrastructure companies get squeezed from both sides — undercut on price by closed labs and undifferentiated on the open-source side by competitors offering the same models. Together AI says it plans to grow its capacity and infrastructure footprint roughly 50-fold over the next five years; that is the window it has to prove the open-model bet is structural rather than a temporary pricing anomaly. That is the wager $800 million and an oil giant's credibility are now riding on.
