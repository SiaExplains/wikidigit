Meta reported record third-quarter revenue of $64.6 billion on Wednesday, October 21, up 26% from a year earlier, and in the same release disclosed that its [free cash flow was negative $2.12 billion](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/meta-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-302915964.html). Capital expenditures of $34.91 billion outran the $32.79 billion the business brought in from operations. A few paragraphs further down, the board announced a new class of publicly listed, nonvoting Class C stock, to be handed to every existing shareholder as a dividend. Investors read the two items together, and on Thursday [Meta shares fell nearly 7%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/meta-stock-q3-earnings-free-cash-flow.html), according to CNBC.

## A Record Quarter That Still Burned Cash

The headline numbers were strong. Ad impressions rose 10% and the average price per ad rose 14%. Family daily active people reached 3.64 billion. Meta guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73–76 billion.

The cost side is where the quarter changed shape. Total costs and expenses rose 68% to $51.6 billion, and operating margin fell from 40% to 20%. Most of that drop comes from a $9.89 billion charge for the August agreement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental health litigation, which provides for payments of up to about $18.59 billion. Without the charge, Meta says operating income would have been $22.88 billion.

The charge explains the margin. It doesn't explain the cash. Capex including finance lease payments came to $34.91 billion in a single quarter, and Meta narrowed its full-year range to $135–145 billion, raising the floor from $130 billion. Across the first nine months, free cash flow was $11.05 billion, down from $29.51 billion a year earlier. Back in May, the company [cut 8,000 jobs while raising this same capex guidance](/article/meta-8000-layoffs-ai-capex); headcount now stands at 66,775, down 15% year over year.

## Debt Now Exceeds Cash

On the earnings call, CFO Susan Li gave the balance-sheet picture in one line: Meta ended the quarter with ["$82.4 billion in cash and marketable securities and $83.7 billion in debt"](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/21/meta-meta-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/). For a company long known for its cash pile, that is a notable crossover, even if the gap is small.

Shareholder returns have already adjusted. Meta bought back no stock in the third quarter and none in the first nine months of 2026, against $26.25 billion of repurchases in the same period of 2025. It still paid $1.36 billion in dividends last quarter.

Meta is not the only AI spender running into its own cash flow. [CoreWeave's worst week on the Nasdaq](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week) showed how fast the market reprices AI infrastructure once debt enters the picture. The difference is that Meta's revenue is still growing at 26%. A week later, [Alphabet's free cash flow went to negative $9.1 billion](/article/alphabet-google-cloud-103-percent-stock-falls) even as Google Cloud revenue doubled.

## What the Class C Stock Actually Does

The board approved a new Class C common stock with no voting rights. Once Meta's amended certificate of incorporation takes effect, expected on January 19, 2027, every Class A and Class B share gets one Class C share as a one-time stock dividend. Meta says Class A shares should trade as if they went through a two-for-one split, that the dividend doesn't change anyone's voting power, and that it plans to list the Class C shares on Nasdaq.

The release lists the uses plainly: capital raising, employee compensation, potential acquisitions and general corporate purposes. A separate Founder Alignment Agreement with Mark Zuckerberg covers how he can sell the Class C shares he receives.

Asked about funding, Li said: "We do not have any specific financing plans to announce at this time." Meta also hasn't said the new stock is a response to the cash flow numbers. The timing is what investors are reacting to. investingLive, reporting the 7.3% midday drop, wrote that the market sees the new class ["as a currency that will dilute the company"](https://investinglive.com/stocks/meta-shares-fall-7-on-huge-spending-plans/) and that it "lets management issue stock without diluting Zuckerberg's voting control."

## The Case Against Panic

There is a reasonable bull reading. Nearly $10 billion of the quarter's costs was a one-time legal charge, operating cash flow still grew from $30.0 billion to $32.8 billion, and Meta still has $82 billion in cash and securities. Reality Labs losses are expected to narrow to about $15 billion in 2027, from $18–19 billion this year. Meta says it expects to "sustain strong overall revenue growth throughout 2027."

What Meta wouldn't give is a number for next year's spending. Li said the company is "quite early in developing our budget and capacity plans for next year" and will share more on the fourth-quarter call. Until then, investors know three things: 2026 capex will land between $135 billion and $145 billion, buybacks have stopped, and Meta has built itself a way to issue shares without touching control. As other AI companies [line up for public markets](/article/openai-ipo-trillion-dollar-filing), Meta is the clearest test yet of how much cash burn shareholders will accept from a company that is still very profitable.
