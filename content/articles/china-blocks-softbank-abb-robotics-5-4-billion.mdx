China has prohibited SoftBank from acquiring ABB's robotics business in the country, putting the $5.375 billion deal at the center of Masayoshi Son's "physical AI" push in doubt. ABB disclosed the decision on November 5, and SoftBank confirmed on November 6 that it received the notice two days earlier. The Financial Times, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that one concern was robotics data leaving China and helping train AI models, including those of OpenAI, where SoftBank is a major investor. The two companies' statements give no reason, and the National Development and Reform Commission did not immediately respond to the FT.

## What ABB and SoftBank Have Confirmed

[ABB's statement](https://www.abb.com/global/en/news/140154/update-on-divestment-of-abb-robotics) says it was informed by SoftBank that the Office of the Working Mechanism for Foreign Investment Security Review in China "has prohibited the acquisition of the ABB Robotics business in China." [SoftBank's notice](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20261106) gives the date, November 4, and says it "will promptly announce any decisions on matters requiring disclosure."

Both statements limit the ban to the business in China, and both say the companies are "working constructively on the transaction." Neither says the wider sale is off. [Reuters](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3373415/china-blocks-softbanks-us54-billion-takeover-abbs-robotics-division) reported that China's state planner told SoftBank it would not allow the acquisition to go ahead. The European Commission had already approved the takeover, according to [Keystone-SDA](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-industry/china-blocks-abbs-sale-of-its-robotics-division-to-softbank/92331115), and ABB declined to tell the AWP news agency anything more about consequences or next steps.

## It Passed One Chinese Review and Failed Another

According to the [FT](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/workplace/china-blocks-softbanks-5-4bn-takeover-of-abbs-robotics-division/92331403), the deal cleared China's competition review in February and then faced a foreign investment security review. People close to SoftBank expected approval until the week before the news broke.

The FT's sources named the NDRC, the state planner responsible for supply chain security, as the body that blocked it. Beijing was wary of transferring China-developed assets to a Japanese company amid tensions with Tokyo and competition with Washington, they said. The review went through ABB's Chinese supply chains in detail, and regulators reportedly demanded that some manufacturing capability stay in the country. They also reportedly considered keeping some robotics intellectual property in China, which could have meant spinning parts of the business off to Chinese owners.

The OpenAI detail comes from anonymous sources, and neither company has addressed it. People close to SoftBank told the FT there was uncertainty in Japan about which conditions Chinese authorities had formally requested before the decision. The ownership link behind the worry is real, though: SoftBank was one of the anchors of [OpenAI's $122 billion round](/article/openai-ipo-trillion-dollar-filing) earlier this year.

## Why the China Business Is Hard to Separate

China is the largest market for ABB's robotics division, and more than 90% of the robots sold to Chinese customers are made there, according to the FT. Shanghai's government helped ABB build a $150 million robotics factory in Pudong, which began operating in 2022. ABB as a whole employs about 12,700 people in China, and it set up a joint venture with a Chinese partner in 1992.

People close to SoftBank told the FT those Chinese operations were a major attraction of the deal. Neither statement puts a number on the China business, which makes a sale without it hard to value from the outside. The whole division [had about 7,000 employees and $2.3 billion in 2024 revenue](https://www.abb.com/global/en/news/129685/abb-to-divest-robotics-division-to-softbank-group), around 7% of ABB Group's total, with a 12.1% operational EBITA margin.

## What Each Company Has Riding on It

ABB expected about $5.3 billion in net cash and a pre-tax book gain of about $2.4 billion when the deal closed, which it had targeted for mid-to-late 2026. Keystone-SDA reported that ABB recently agreed to buy British automation specialist Rotork for $5.5 billion, and that CEO Morten Wierod referred to the expected robotics proceeds when discussing it.

"SoftBank's next frontier is Physical AI," Son said when the deal was announced. The FT reported that the business was meant to anchor that plan alongside the AI and robotics company Roze in the US, and that Son is also in talks to buy security group Allied Universal to boost its productivity with robotics and AI.

SoftBank shares have fallen 38% in the past month, per the FT, on concerns about AI demand and OpenAI's decision to delay its IPO to next year. The FT said that delay also threatens the planned listing of SB Energy, SoftBank's US data center developer, at a target valuation of $50 billion.

## The Second Big Deal China Has Stopped This Year

Reuters called this the second major deal blocked by China in 2026, after Meta was ordered to unwind its $2 billion-plus purchase of the AI start-up Manus. It said the news comes as Beijing tightens scrutiny of foreign investment in domestic start-ups building frontier technology. ABB doesn't fit that description neatly, since it is a Swiss group selling to a Japanese one, and its Chinese operations go back to at least 1992.

Beijing has also said no elsewhere in this market. Chinese firms [pulled back from Nvidia H200 chips](/article/nvidia-h200-china-refuses-us-approved-chips) that Washington had approved, after guidance from Beijing. And if the data concern is accurate, it fits how investors already talk about Chinese robotics: JPMorgan's [upgrade of Unitree](/article/unitree-h2x-robot-lost-leg-jpmorgan-double-upgrade) last month cited robots, developers and real-world data forming a cycle.

The FT reported SoftBank is trying to work out what went wrong and whether it can apply again. Neither company has announced a change to the deal's scope, and ABB's original target for closing, mid-to-late 2026, has less than two months left to run.
