On October 22, at Unitree Robotics' own World Physical AI Conference in Hangzhou, the company's day-old H2-X "Kung Fu Legend Guardian" humanoid attempted a side flip on stage, landed badly and lost its right lower leg at the joint. Four days later, on October 26, JPMorgan upgraded Unitree two notches, from Underweight to Overweight, and raised its price target from 300 yuan to 995 yuan. The shares closed up 7.69% at 672 yuan on Shanghai's STAR Market. Both came out of the same event, and the bank was not looking at the stunts.

## The Robot That Broke a Leg One Day After Launch

Unitree introduced the H2-X on October 21 with a one-minute video on X that drew about 15,000 likes. It is a variant of the $29,900 H2 humanoid with a claimed eight hours of battery life, versus roughly three for the standard H2, and the footage shows it kicking car doors, confronting a knife-wielding intruder and lifting a vehicle to free a trapped person. Unitree pitched it for security patrols and emergency rescue.

According to [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/10/26/unitrees-newly-launched-h2-x-robot-loses-a-leg-in-public-demonstration/), the H2-X attempted a side flip, lost its balance on landing, fell sideways, and its right mechanical lower leg detached completely at the joint. [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202610/1372914.shtml) reported mixed reactions on Weibo and said Unitree did not reply when contacted on Sunday. As of October 26 the company had not explained the cause. No price or release date has been announced, and the H2-X is not in mass production.

## What Wang Xingxing Said Instead

The H2-X was a sideshow. Founder and CEO Wang Xingxing spent his speech, more than 90% of it by the estimate of a Phoenix Tech column republished by [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4038265538581382), on AI, and used it to shorten his own forecast. In August at the World Robot Conference in Beijing, he said mass use of humanoids in factories and homes could take up to ten years. In Hangzhou he said a physical AI breakthrough could come in one to two years at the fastest, two to three at the slowest, and that embodied intelligence could have its "ChatGPT moment" as early as 2027.

He also set a concrete bar. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/unitree-founder-now-expects-robots-breakthrough-as-soon-as-2028) reported that Wang now expects humanoids to complete 80% of voice-assigned tasks, across factory and household work, as soon as 2028. That threshold is his own definition of the "aha" moment, not an industry benchmark. He was candid about today: "If you take a humanoid home now and ask it to clean the room, honestly I don't think it can complete that task," he said, according to a transcript reported by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/unitree-rehearsal-self-improving-robots-2028-breakthrough).

The centrepiece was Unitree Rehearsal, which Wang called the company's most important project of the year: a loop, as reported by SCMP and The Next Web, in which AI searches research papers, writes robot control code, tests it in simulation and then runs trials on physical robots. Humans still score the outcomes; Wang's long-term goal is for that oversight to become optional. The Phoenix Tech column noted that Wang called self-evolution a pre-research project, and that the most striking demos, a humanoid painting and playing Go, ran on OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra rather than Unitree's own models.

## Why JPMorgan Changed Its Mind

JPMorgan had been a bear. According to [Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/122946), the bank previously argued that Unitree and other Chinese robot makers leaned too heavily on hardware and were weak in software and developer platforms. After hearing Wang and reviewing the humanoid lineup in Hangzhou, it reversed. The note said "Unitree is further enhancing its strategic value as robots, developers and real-world data form an ecosystem cycle," and that the AI data bottleneck has eased significantly.

[Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/unitree-robotics-shares-surge-on-jpmorgan-doubleupgrade-4971467) reported that Wang's comments moved JPMorgan's own view of the breakthrough timeline to one to two years from up to ten, and that the bank raised its sales forecast on an "accelerated adoption curve," with projections for 2028 and 2030 implying 10% to 20% global market share. Futu News reported that the 995 yuan target is for December 2027 and that the upward revision is concentrated after 2028, with the 2026 humanoid sales forecast unchanged. The upgrade is a bet on the roadmap, not on anything shipping this year.

The market had already moved. On October 23, the day after the conference, Unitree hit its first 20% daily limit-up since listing and closed at 624 yuan, a market value of about 252.4 billion yuan, according to [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/2bc25789-2b22-42e4-bdd3-5584082cba0e). The humanoid robot concept index closed up 4.92% that day. On October 26 the stock rose as much as 11% intraday to 693.50 yuan, a two-month high, before closing at 672.

## A Stock That Has Done This Before

Unitree is the first humanoid maker listed on mainland China's exchanges. It priced its IPO at 150.8 yuan and opened on August 19 at 1,100 yuan, a 629% gain, before closing the day around 845. By October 15 it had slid to an intraday low of 404.3 yuan. The October 23 close was a 54% rebound from that low, and still well below the debut.

The valuation remains extreme. BigGo put the trailing price-to-earnings ratio at about 388 times, against an industry average near 40. The Phoenix Tech column added that Unitree's total R&D spending for 2023 to 2025 was about 265 million yuan, not comparable to AI model companies, and that its self-developed model, UnifoLM-WLA-1.0, got no live demo in Hangzhou.

That is the tension. The leg is a hardware problem, and hardware is what Unitree is known for. JPMorgan's upgrade rests on software Unitree calls pre-research, demonstrated largely with someone else's model. It is the same gap as when [Agility Robotics went public on $1.8 million of revenue](/article/agility-robotics-2-5b-spac-1-8m-revenue): investors are paying for robots promised for 2027 and 2028, not the ones on stage. And as [CoreWeave learned after Jensen Huang's endorsement](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week), a big name's vote of confidence is not a floor.

Unitree's first quarterly report since listing is due October 29: the first number, rather than a forecast, since the leg came off.
