WeRide, the Guangzhou autonomous driving company listed in New York and Hong Kong, said on November 20 that its robotaxi revenue passed RMB100 million in a single quarter for the first time, up 204% from a year earlier. More than 90% of that money came from outside China, led by its fleets in the Middle East. The [third-quarter results](https://ir.weride.ai/news-releases/news-release-details/overseas-revenue-1715-yoy-contributing-nearly-half-total-revenue) describe a Chinese robotaxi company that now earns its robotaxi revenue almost entirely abroad, and they came out the same week Waymo pulled its cars off freeways in every US market it serves.

## The Numbers Behind the Headline

Total revenue for the quarter ended September 30 was RMB321.5 million (US$48.0 million), up 88% from RMB171.0 million a year earlier and up 38.8% from the second quarter. Gross margin rose to 43.0% from 32.9%.

Overseas revenue was RMB146.9 million (US$21.9 million), up 171.5% and equal to 45.7% of the total. Robotaxis are only part of that picture. WeRide also sells L2++/L3 driver-assistance software to carmakers, and cumulative deliveries of its WRD 3.0 system passed 100,000 units. A data unit called Jingshuo, which sells training data to robotics and foundation-model companies, nearly doubled its revenue. The robotaxi line grew fastest of the three, though, and it is the one that depends on foreign cities.

CEO Tony Han said in the release that overseas markets now generate "nearly half of our total revenue and more than 90% of our Robotaxi revenue."

## Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Uber Do the Heavy Lifting

The Middle East fleet reached about 600 vehicles, up roughly 50% from about 400 at the end of July. WeRide wants to cover the core urban areas of Abu Dhabi and Dubai by year-end. On the earnings call, [transcribed by The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/20/weride-wrd-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/), Han said he expects that fleet to nearly double next year.

A lot of this runs through Uber. WeRide says it is Uber's largest autonomous vehicle partner outside the United States by the number of fully driverless robotaxis on Uber's platform. In September, WeRide, Uber and AVOMO got Spain's first national L4 operating permit for autonomous passenger vehicles from the Spanish traffic authority, and mapping around Madrid has started ahead of their first joint launch in Europe.

WeRide doesn't buy the cars abroad. It supplies the driving technology and remote assistance, and local partners pay for the fleet and run the day-to-day operations. The company credits that arrangement for the higher margin on its overseas revenue.

Europe is smaller. WeRide opened public robotaxi rides in the Canton of Zurich with Swiss rail operator SBB and Swiss Transit Lab, its Robobus at Zurich Airport now runs with no safety operator on board, and its first robotaxis started road testing in Slovakia. In Singapore, the service it runs with Grab had given more than 20,000 L4 rides by the end of September. CFO Jennifer Li said WeRide operates in 13 countries and holds permits in 9.

## Guangzhou Finally Covers Its Costs

At home, WeRide said its Guangzhou robotaxi operations reached positive unit economics in the quarter. Domestic robotaxis averaged 21 orders per vehicle per day, with a one-day peak of 33, and cumulative registered users rose 143.5% from the end of the previous quarter.

As of October 31, the global L4 fleet was about 4,100 vehicles, including more than 2,300 robotaxis. The robotaxi fleet grew 26% from the previous quarter. Even with Guangzhou breaking even per car, China still accounts for less than a tenth of robotaxi revenue.

## Still Losing RMB310 Million a Quarter

WeRide is still far from profitable. According to its [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001867729/000110465926130438/tm2630019d1_ex99-1.htm), it lost RMB310.0 million (US$46.2 million) in the quarter, close to the RMB307.3 million it lost a year earlier. Cash, deposits and short-term investments totaled RMB4.66 billion (US$695.5 million) at the end of September.

Li said that gives WeRide "more than four years of cash runway," and Han told analysts the money fully funds a plan to reach positive quarterly free cash flow in 2028. Investors are paying up for robotics companies with much less to show: [Agility Robotics went public at a $2.5 billion valuation on $1.8 million of 2025 sales](/article/agility-robotics-2-5b-spac-1-8m-revenue), and [JPMorgan double-upgraded Unitree days after its robot lost a leg on stage](/article/unitree-h2x-robot-lost-leg-jpmorgan-double-upgrade). WeRide can at least point to RMB100 million a quarter from paid rides.

## The Same Week Waymo Pulled Off the Freeway

On November 11, California's DMV barred Waymo from the state's freeways, saying repeated incursions into active construction zones "pose an imminent hazard to public safety," [The Robot Report reported](https://www.therobotreport.com/california-dmv-bars-waymo-avs-from-operating-on-the-states-freeways/). Waymo then restricted freeway driving in all its markets, while noting that its surface-street service still handles more than half a million trips a week.

Han calls permits WeRide's moat: "Permits take years to secure and represent one of the hardest-to-replicate moats in this business." California just showed that a regulator can also narrow one overnight. WeRide's robotaxi revenue now depends mostly on officials in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, with Zurich, Singapore and Madrid behind them, so its growth will follow how those governments treat driverless cars. Regulators have already reshaped other robotics deals this year, as when [China blocked SoftBank's $5.4 billion purchase of ABB Robotics](/article/china-blocks-softbank-abb-robotics-5-4-billion).
