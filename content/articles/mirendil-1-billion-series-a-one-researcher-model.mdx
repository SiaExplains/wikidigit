Mirendil, a San Francisco AI lab started by two former Anthropic researchers, announced a [$1 billion Series A](https://mirendil.com/news/series-a/) on November 12, co-led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital. The money came less than five months after a $200 million seed round. Mirendil's headline claim is that a single researcher working with AI agents pretrained its competitive coding model, Mirendil-Base, from scratch in 3.5 months for under $10 million in compute. Battery Ventures, Doerr Capital, Gil & Co, Google, M13, Pear VC and S32 also joined the round. Mirendil hasn't disclosed a valuation, though Bloomberg reported in September that it was in talks to raise up to $1 billion at $5 billion.

## Five Months From Seed to $1 Billion

Mirendil came out in June with a [$200 million seed round](https://thenextweb.com/news/mirendil-200m-seed-ai-research) co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Kleiner Perkins at a $1 billion valuation. Nvidia joined too. At the time it had no product and promised a model and product "in the coming months."

Three months later, [Bloomberg reported](https://dealroom.co/news/155841-ex-anthropic-founders-mirendil-eyes-5b-valuation-months-after-1b-seed/) on September 22 that Kleiner Perkins was in talks to lead a new round of up to $1 billion at a $5 billion valuation, with Andreessen Horowitz also in discussions. The round that closed matches the size Bloomberg described. Kleiner led it, this time alongside Clearlake. Neither Mirendil's announcement nor [SiliconANGLE's report](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/13/mirendil-raises-1b-in-funding-to-speed-up-ai-model-development/) gives a price, so the $5 billion figure is still the reported talks and not a confirmed number.

A month earlier, another young lab, Flapping Airplanes, [raised $1 billion at a reported $6 billion](/article/flapping-airplanes-1-billion-6-billion-no-product) without a product. Mirendil goes into its round with three named products, though none is generally available.

## The Founders Left Anthropic in December

CEO Behnam Neyshabur spent more than five years at Google co-leading reasoning research for Gemini, according to [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mirendil-200m-seed-ai-research). SiliconANGLE notes he co-invented SAM, an algorithm that helps models do better on tasks they weren't specifically trained for. CTO Harsh Mehta built the first version of Anthropic's internal AI research platform. The two met at Google in 2019, joined Anthropic in late 2024 and left in December 2025.

At the seed stage the team was about 20 researchers and engineers from Anthropic, xAI, Google DeepMind and OpenAI. Neyshabur describes the focus as "AI for AI for science": tools that speed up AI research itself, which then speeds up everything else. Andreessen Horowitz partner Matt Bornstein told The Wall Street Journal at the time that "structurally, there has to be an independent company" doing this work.

## What One Researcher and a Swarm of Agents Built

Most attention has gone to how Mirendil-Base was made. The company says one researcher led the project without help from colleagues. According to SiliconANGLE, agents wrote more than 99% of the code, set up the infrastructure, chose a training algorithm, collected web training data and refined the dataset and architecture. The whole thing took about 3.5 months and cost around $10 million. Mirendil's own post says compute came in under $10 million.

All of this comes from Mirendil. No outside group has reproduced the process, and the announcement doesn't publish the benchmarks behind "competitive." If it holds up, it's a big change, since pretraining a model to compete with leading open-source LLMs is usually a job for a team of specialists.

Mirendil now has three named products:

- Lor, in private beta, runs hundreds of agents that write code and run experiments for days at a time. SiliconANGLE says it automates trial-and-error comparison of algorithm variants and fixes errors that come up during evaluation. Mirendil says it runs on Lor daily.
- Mirendil-Base, the agent-built pretrained model.
- Fern, a post-trained coding model unveiled in October on a Mirendil-Base version with 100 billion to 500 billion parameters. It accepts up to 10 million tokens of context, which SiliconANGLE says is ten times Claude Opus 5.5 and roughly half a million lines of code.

Late in October the company also previewed two larger Mirendil-Base versions with 500 billion to 10 trillion parameters.

## Why Investors Are Paying for Small Teams

Mirendil's announcement states the thesis directly: "when agents do the hands-on work, the size of your team stops limiting what you can build." The company calls itself "a small team" and doesn't give a headcount.

Other parts of the industry are heading the same way. GitHub said agents were drafting [more than 40% of its pull requests](/article/github-octoverse-agents-40-percent-pull-requests) by September. Recursive Superintelligence [raised $650 million in May](/article/recursive-superintelligence-650m-self-improving-ai) on the idea of AI that improves itself. Mirendil is selling a narrower version of that idea: agents that do the grunt work of AI research while people choose what to try.

The open question is whether Lor works for anyone outside Mirendil. It's still in private beta, the company has disclosed no customers or revenue, and the one-researcher story comes from Mirendil itself. Kleiner Perkins backed the company at the seed stage and led again five months later, and Google joined this time. For anyone outside that group, the first real test is when Lor leaves private beta.
