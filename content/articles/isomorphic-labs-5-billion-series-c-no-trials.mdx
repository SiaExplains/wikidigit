Isomorphic Labs, the London drug-discovery company Demis Hassabis spun out of Google DeepMind in 2021, announced a $5 billion Series C on November 17, co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital. [BioSpace reports](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/isomorphic-labs-breaks-biotech-vc-fundraising-record-with-5b-series-c) that it is the largest single private round biotech has seen, yet the company still has no drug in human trials and has not publicly named a single asset. The investors are backing a software engine that designs molecules, and none of those molecules has been tested in a person yet.

## Who Wrote the $5 Billion Check

Greenoaks and Sequoia led. Andreessen Horowitz came in as a new investor alongside several others, and Alphabet, Isomorphic's parent, put in more money. According to the [company's announcement](https://www.bioindustry.org/resource/isomorphic-labs-announces-5-billion-series-c-funding.html), every existing investor returned: Thrive Capital, MGX, GV, CapitalG, Temasek and the UK Sovereign AI Fund.

Isomorphic did not give a valuation. Bloomberg reported earlier this month that the company was in talks to raise around $5 billion at roughly $50 billion, a figure [pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/isomorphic-labs-raises-5bn-massive-third-round) also cites as its estimated paper value.

Sequoia partner Konstantine Buhler, one of the seven general partners who now run the firm after its [recent shake-up and roughly $10 billion raise](/article/sequoia-10-billion-closes-london-arc-scouts), called using AI to improve human health "one of the most important challenges of our generation." Greenoaks founder Neil Mehta described drug development as "an optimization challenge of almost infinite scale."

## How Fast the Money Arrived

Isomorphic took its first outside money, $600 million, in March 2025. It raised a $2.1 billion Series B in May 2026. Six months later, the Series C is about 2.4 times that. BioSpace counts at least $7.7 billion raised in total.

The previous biotech record belonged to Altos Labs, which launched with $3 billion in 2022. Isomorphic has now beaten that in a single round, and its May raise alone came within $900 million of it.

## What the Engine Actually Does

Isomorphic's product, for now, is a platform it calls IsoDDE, its drug design engine. Pharmaphorum describes it as a set of foundational simulation AI models that predict 3D protein structures, how small molecules interact with them, how tightly they bind, and where the binding "pockets" sit. The company says the models work across several therapeutic areas and drug types.

Hassabis's pitch is speed. "Our drug design engine is generating molecules with unprecedented precision and speed," he said in the announcement. President Max Jaderberg said, "Every week we are making repeatable scientific advancements enabled by our models."

The company has research partnerships with Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson. None of the coverage gives deal values for them.

According to the announcement, the money will scale IsoDDE, expand it into new areas of biology, build a partner network for disease research, and build "AI-first translational and clinical development infrastructure" as candidates move toward trials. That last item means manufacturing preparation, regulatory work and running clinical programs, the stages of drug development Isomorphic has not reached yet.

## The Trial That Hasn't Started

In January 2025, Hassabis told Bloomberg at Davos: "We'll hopefully have some AI-designed drugs in clinical trials by the end of the year." He added, "That's the plan."

That didn't happen. Pharmaphorum reports that Isomorphic has not yet moved any drug candidate into clinical trials. BioSpace notes the company has not publicly disclosed any assets and gave no start date for its first trial. What it has said is that internal programs in oncology and immunology, plus some partnered programs, have produced positive preclinical results, "setting the stage for clinical entry." Hassabis said the round will help drive "our first therapeutic programs towards clinical trials."

Preclinical data comes before any human testing, and the road from there to an approved drug runs through several phases of trials. Even a late-stage result can go badly for a company, as Moderna found when [its melanoma vaccine's Phase 3 data leaked early and the stock fell 17%](/article/moderna-melanoma-vaccine-data-leak-stock-fell-17-percent).

## Why Investors Pay Anyway

This round fits a pattern WikiDigit has tracked all autumn: very large checks written ahead of any shipped product. In October, [Flapping Airplanes raised $1 billion at a reported $6 billion valuation](/article/flapping-airplanes-1-billion-6-billion-no-product) without having launched anything. Isomorphic has more to point to, with a Nobel-winning founder and three big-pharma partners, but in both cases investors are pricing what the technology might produce.

The next milestone is the one Hassabis first promised for 2025: a first patient dosed with an Isomorphic-designed drug. The company has not said when that will happen. Until it does, a valuation Bloomberg puts near $50 billion rests on preclinical results from programs the company has not named publicly.
