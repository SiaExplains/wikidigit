Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday, November 19, after raising $79 billion at $83 a share, the largest IPO ever by money raised. The stock opened at $111.50, up 34%, and closed at $97.17, up 17%, on a day when the Nasdaq, the S&P 500 and the Dow all closed more than 2% lower. The record raise didn't stop the rest of the AI trade from selling off around it.

## The Deal Got Bigger Every Day

Anthropic marketed the shares at $70 to $78 and priced them on Wednesday night at $83, above the range. Fortune reported that the offering was upsized to 980 million shares from 825 million, and that seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, agreed to buy $26 billion of nonvoting stock at the IPO price. Bloomberg, citing underwriters it did not name, said the book was as much as seven times oversubscribed and only about half of institutional orders were filled.

That added up to $79 billion in new money, or $81.4 billion including shares sold by insiders, according to Fortune. SpaceX raised $75 billion in its June IPO, so Anthropic now holds the record for capital raised. SpaceX keeps the valuation record: it listed at $1.77 trillion, against Anthropic's $1.5 trillion at the offer price. If underwriters use their option on another 147 million shares, Fortune says proceeds would reach $118 billion.

Anthropic also reserved about 20% of the offering for individual investors, and Vanda Research estimated about $250 million of retail buying, double what it saw after SpaceX's debut, NBC News reported. NBC also talked to one retail investor who asked for six shares, got one, and bought four more around $97.

## From $2 Trillion at the Open to $1.76 Trillion at the Close

Trading opened at 11:50 a.m. ET at $111.50, a market value of roughly $2 trillion. It didn't hold. CNBC had the stock up about 27%, near $105, during the afternoon, and it closed at $97.17. NBC put the fully diluted market value at the close at about $1.76 trillion. After the bell the shares fell again, by 2% according to CNBC and by more than 2.5% according to NBC.

The broader market had a worse day. The Nasdaq opened up as much as 1.9% before reversing, and it finished down about 550 points, with the Dow off 1,101 points, Fortune reported. NBC said tech weakness came in part from worries that Nvidia's China export problems reach further into the AI supply chain. The week before, Jensen Huang [resigned as Nvidia's CEO](/article/nvidia-huang-resigns-kress-earnings-five-days), which Fortune cited as part of the backdrop for the offering. CNBC summed it up: the IPO's novelty was not enough to lift the market or the rest of the AI trade.

Worries about AI spending have hit the market before: in October, CoreWeave led an [AI debt selloff](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week) that gave the Nasdaq a bad week. Two days after the listing, the selloff reached crypto too, when [bitcoin fell to its lowest price since September 2024](/article/bitcoin-55326-lowest-since-2024-rebound-60000).

## What Buyers Are Paying For

In the nine months to September 30, it reported $33.2 billion in revenue, up from $2.5 billion a year earlier, according to Fortune. NBC noted that the company had $41 million in revenue in all of 2023. It also reported a $104 billion net loss over those nine months, which Fortune says came mostly from how Anthropic accounts for convertible notes. CNBC has reported that the company carries $900 billion in spending commitments.

Dario Amodei, Anthropic's CEO, pitched the long view from the Nasdaq. "I believe sufficiently powerful AI, working in concert with humans, could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade," he said, according to NBC, while also saying the technology carries "very real risks." In a statement quoted by Fortune, he said the listing "puts the company in a stronger financial position." Wolfe Research, Citizens and Melius Research started coverage with buy ratings, Fortune reported.

Insiders can't cash out all at once. NBC reported that employees can sell 10% of their shares now and up to another 35% after fiscal-year earnings are reported, before the end of March 2027. NBC and Fortune, citing CNBC, put the number of staff holding stakes worth more than $100 million at 400 to 600.

## OpenAI Is Watching

Anthropic is the first pure-play frontier model maker to go public, and its rival is still private. OpenAI [filed confidentially for its own IPO](/article/openai-ipo-trillion-dollar-filing) in June, but according to NBC, Sam Altman called a 2026 listing "ill-advised" in September, and Fortune reports OpenAI is waiting until at least 2027. "It's going to help OpenAI become public," Eddie Best of Willkie Farr & Gallagher told NBC.

Rebecca Walser of Walser Wealth Management called the IPO "the show me moment for the AI story," in comments to NBC. On day one, buyers paid $97 a share for a company that lost $104 billion in nine months, mostly on paper, and the next real check is its first earnings report as a public company.
