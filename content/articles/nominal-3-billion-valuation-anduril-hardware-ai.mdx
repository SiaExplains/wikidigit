Nominal, an Austin startup that sells AI software to engineers who build aircraft, reactors and factory equipment, [raised $240 million](https://nominal.io/blog/series-c-news) at a $3 billion valuation on November 9. That is three times the $1 billion it was valued at in March, a round that [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/ai-hardware-startup-nominal-triples-valuation-to-3b-in-8-months/) was led by Founders Fund partner Trae Stephens, a co-founder of Anduril. Anduril is also one of Nominal's largest customers, and Nominal's CEO used to work there.

## Who Paid $3 Billion

Lightspeed Venture Partners led the Series C and Insight Partners co-led it. The [company's announcement](https://nominal.io/blog/series-c-news) lists a long tail behind them: Lux Capital, Sequoia, General Catalyst, Founders Fund, Avenir, Lockheed Martin Ventures, Salesforce Ventures, M13, DE Shaw Ventures and a dozen smaller funds.

Lockheed Martin Ventures stands out on that list. Nominal says five of the seven largest defense contractors use its platform, and Lockheed's venture arm is now an investor as well.

The March round was described by TechCrunch as preemptive, meaning investors came to Nominal with an offer before the company went looking. Reports disagree on how much money changed hands in it. [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/nominal-raises-240m-at-3b-valuation-tripling-in-eight-months-to-put-ai-inside-hardware-engineering/) puts it at $80 million, while TechCrunch describes a $155 million Series B extension. Both agree on the $1 billion price and on Founders Fund leading. Before that, Sequoia led a $75 million Series B, according to Tech Funding News.

Nominal hasn't disclosed revenue, headcount or total funding raised, and it hasn't said whether $3 billion is the pre-money or post-money figure.

## What Nominal Actually Sells

Nominal's customers build things like aircraft, nuclear reactors and automated factories, and testing them produces a lot of sensor data that engineers have to read against the design and the requirements. The platform puts the raw data and that engineering context in one place. It runs in the cloud, on a customer's own servers, in air-gapped environments or at the edge, which is the range a defense contractor needs.

On top of that sits Nominal Analyst, the company's first AI agent. It investigates engineering data, runs analyses and traces every answer back to the evidence it came from, while engineers keep the reviews and approvals. "Engineers stay in command, with purpose-built AI helping them do their best work," CEO Cameron McCord said in the announcement. Developers can also reach the data through SQL, APIs and the Model Context Protocol, so teams can wire it into their own tools and agents.

Nominal backed the pitch with customer numbers. At Anduril, more than 300 engineers use the platform, and analysis that used to take five to six hours now runs in near real time. A pilot at shipbuilder HII cut some analysis tasks from hours to minutes and roughly halved some production test steps. Hermeus cut the time from data upload to review by 80% while it designed and built its Quarterhorse Mk 1 aircraft in seven months. All three figures come from Nominal's announcement; the customers haven't published their own.

## The Anduril Thread

McCord is a former US Navy submarine officer who worked at Anduril before co-founding Nominal in 2022 with Bryce Strauss and Jason Hoch, who also come from the Navy, defense and enterprise software. Stephens co-founded Anduril, and as a Founders Fund partner he led the round that took Nominal to $1 billion. Anduril, in turn, is the customer with the biggest usage number in Nominal's announcement.

Defense tech is a small world, and overlaps like this are common. Still, Nominal's biggest usage number comes from a company closely tied to one of its investors. The newer support comes from further out. Lightspeed and Insight, neither of them part of the Anduril story, led this round, and in October the US Air Force picked Nominal for a $71.2 million Strategic Funding Increase to support Collaborative Combat Aircraft work. TechCrunch notes that the program requires matching money from outside the Air Force.

The pattern of AI money flowing toward physical hardware has been visible all year, from [Tenstorrent's $1.5 billion Series E](/article/tenstorrent-1-5-billion-series-e-keller-independent) to [Agility Robotics heading to Nasdaq on a $2.5 billion SPAC](/article/agility-robotics-2-5b-spac-1-8m-revenue). Nominal doesn't build any of that hardware. It sells software to the engineers who do.

## Where the $240 Million Goes

Nominal says the money will fund more agents and applications covering requirements verification, planning, scheduling and fleet health across a machine's life. It also plans to move into new industries and regions, partly by buying companies. It has already made two acquisitions this year: Fid Labs, whose team builds Nominal's agents, and Pull Systems, which takes the platform into automotive. Tech Funding News adds robotics and energy to the list of target industries.

"Hardware engineering is getting its AI moment, with real operational value," McCord said. Investors have now paid three times March's price for that view in eight months, without seeing a revenue figure.
