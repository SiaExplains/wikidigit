Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and board member on Thursday, November 12, effective immediately, ending a 33-year run at the company he co-founded. Five days later, on Tuesday, November 17, CFO Colette Kress will present Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter results as interim CEO. Nvidia guided to $108 billion in revenue for the quarter, and it will be the company's first report since Huang left.

## Four Days From Apology to Exit

On Sunday, November 8, Huang sent employees a message admitting he had been "unfaithful" to his wife. "My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry," he wrote, according to ABC News. The same day, Nvidia's board said it had confidence in his leadership but opened an independent investigation into reports that he "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." CNBC reported that the board hired Wachtell Lipton to run it.

The woman at the center of those reports is Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang. She is a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that is an Nvidia customer. The New York Times reported that US surveillance suggested she passed information to Beijing. None of it has been proven in public. China called the allegations "baseless and purely slanderous smears against China," and on November 15 Megaspeed said she left the company in 2025, denied any wrongdoing and said it "conducts legitimate and lawful business." Nvidia's board has said it has no reason to believe national security was compromised.

According to CNBC, a second report is what tipped the board. On Wednesday, November 11, the New York Times reported that Huang had also had an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reported to him. A person familiar with the matter told CNBC the board decided that night to push him out. Thursday morning's release thanked him ("Jensen's contributions to NVIDIA are profound") and gave no reason for his departure.

## Kress Takes Over With Two Jobs

Kress has been Nvidia's CFO since September 2013, after senior finance roles at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco. Over her tenure, Nvidia's annual revenue went from $4.13 billion in fiscal 2014 to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, Al Jazeera notes. She keeps the CFO title while serving as interim CEO, so the person explaining the quarter's numbers on Tuesday is also the person answering for the company's direction.

The board also named Harvey C. Jones as chair, a post CNBC described as the company's first-ever board chair. "The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team," Jones said. The search for a permanent CEO will cover internal and external candidates. Analysts have floated insiders such as Ian Buck, Bryan Catanzaro, Michael Kagan and Bill Dally. Donald Trump went further on Truth Social and wrote that "there is only one man to take his place - TIM COOK of Apple."

## What the Market Did

Nvidia shares fell more than 13% at Thursday's open, pushing the company below a $4 trillion market value, then recovered to close down 4.3%, CNBC reported. The stock had closed at $173.73 on Wednesday, already well under its October 6 closing high of $239.24. Retail traders bought on the dip: Vanda Research counted a net $111 million of Nvidia buying from individuals in the first 30 minutes.

The selling spread to the companies whose businesses lean on Nvidia's. CoreWeave fell between 9% and 12% during the session and Nebius dropped 17% to 19%, while Broadcom rose more than 5% and AMD about 3%. CoreWeave was already under pressure from [worries about the debt behind the AI data-center buildout](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week). Nvidia is its biggest supplier.

Most analysts cut their price targets. Baird downgraded the stock to neutral and cut its target from $500 to $200. Stifel cut from $315 to $205 but kept a buy rating, and Cantor Fitzgerald went from $350 to $250.

## What Tuesday's Numbers Have to Show

Nvidia will report after the US close on November 17, with the call at 5pm New York time. Its own guidance for the quarter is $108 billion in revenue, plus or minus 2%, with an adjusted gross margin of about 74%. Bloomberg consensus, cited by Saxo, sits at roughly $109.1 billion in revenue, up about 91% year on year, and adjusted earnings of about $2.47 per share.

Saxo's preview puts more weight on the fourth-quarter outlook than on the reported quarter. Investors will also listen for Blackwell demand, the Rubin ramp and anything Kress says about China. Nvidia already left China data-center compute sales out of its third-quarter outlook, a market that has been politically difficult for months, as Beijing showed when it [told its own companies not to buy US-approved H200 chips](/article/nvidia-h200-china-refuses-us-approved-chips). Saxo also flags the open questions around Megaspeed and possible diversion of restricted chips, and notes that none of them are findings of wrongdoing.

Nvidia's commitments reach well beyond its own results. As of July, it had $36 billion in backstops for neocloud providers and $20 billion in data-center leases, according to its 10-Q, and it plans to buy $10 billion of Anthropic stock after Anthropic's IPO, which is set to start trading on November 19.

## Washington Isn't Finished

CNBC reported that he has agreed to testify before a House committee, with Rep. Brian Mast saying he would subpoena him if needed. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott has asked Huang to appear on December 3 and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on December 10. Sen. Ron Wyden and Rep. Richard Neal have opened a probe into Nvidia's export approvals, and Rosen Law Firm has filed a proposed shareholder class action.
