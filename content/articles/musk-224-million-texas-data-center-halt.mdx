Elon Musk spent about $224 million backing Republicans in Texas this cycle, and on Tuesday Democrats won both the governor's office and the Senate seat there. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa wants new data center projects halted, and SpaceX's revenue has soared on renting out AI computing. SpaceX and Tesla shares fell before the US open on Wednesday while Dow futures jumped.

Hinojosa unseated Greg Abbott, becoming the first Democrat [elected Texas governor in more than three decades](https://investing.com/news/stock-market-news/elon-musks-business-empire-braces-for-democratic-wave-as-texas-flips-blue-4998814), according to Reuters. James Talarico beat Ken Paxton, and Newsweek says he is [the first Democrat to win a Texas Senate race since 1988](https://www.newsweek.com/elon-musk-donate-republican-campaigns-midterms-elections-2026-12660475). Democrats also won both chambers of Congress.

## SpaceX and Tesla Fell While Dow Futures Jumped

The first move came on Robinhood's overnight market, where [SpaceX was down more than 6% and Tesla more than 4%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) as Hinojosa extended her lead. By premarket, Reuters had SpaceX down 3.8% and Tesla down 3.6%. Business Insider, reporting both down more than 4%, calculated that [the two could lose more than $140 billion in combined value](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tesla-spacex-shares-drop-elon-110520141.html) if those declines held at the open.

The wider market went the other way. Dow futures were up 912 points, or 1.75%, at 8:08 a.m. ET, and S&P 500 futures were up 0.8%. Nasdaq-100 futures lagged on weakness in AI-related stocks, after touching their lowest level since July 30 overnight. CoreWeave, [which lost about a quarter of its value in one week of AI debt fears](/article/coreweave-ai-debt-selloff-nasdaq-worst-week) in October, is down about 41% over the past month, per CNBC.

All of these are pre-open figures, since regular trading had not started when this was published.

## America PAC's Money Mostly Went to Losing Races

CNBC's review of campaign finance records found Musk spent [at least $474 million on the 2026 elections](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/elon-musk-midterms-ai-data-centers.html), with $224 million of it in Texas. Other tallies run lower: Newsweek counts more than $445 million through October 14. The New York Times, cited by Business Insider, says $158 million of the Texas money came in September alone.

His America PAC spent nearly $87 million across 44 federal races. As of Wednesday morning, the Republicans it backed had lost 29 of those contests and won three, with 12 uncalled. CNBC says more than $80 million of that spending, or 93%, went to races Republicans lost.

Musk's public reaction was a joke on X: "Guess my election-interfering space lasers didn't work this time." Reuters reports he had urged followers to vote Republican as recently as Tuesday and later deleted that post.

## Why a Texas Pause Would Hit SpaceX's Growth Plan

SpaceX has become a [compute landlord for the AI industry](/article/spacex-colossus-ai-compute-landlord). Reuters says renting capacity to customers including Anthropic sent revenue soaring and helped it post its first profit as a public company. Capacity stood at 1.7 GW at the end of September. The company plans as much as 3 GW by year-end and 13 GW next year.

Colossus and Colossus II sit near Memphis, so a Texas pause would not touch them directly. SemiAnalysis has identified more than 9 GW of planned or developing SpaceX capacity, including facilities at Starbase in Boca Chica, Texas. Wells Fargo estimates Texas holds about 27% of US data center gigawatts, so a pause would land well beyond Musk.

Hinojosa has gone after Musk specifically as well. She sued to obtain emails between him and Abbott, accused Abbott of offering him favorable deals, and opposed a potential sale of a beachfront state park to SpaceX, saying "Texas is not for sale."

Musk's answer so far: "Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now." Reuters notes that orbital data centers still face cooling and repair challenges.

## Washington Adds a Second Front

Robert Garcia, the incoming House Oversight chair, told Republicans and their allies to "Lawyer up." Reuters says Musk threatened retaliation on X last month after a report that Democrats were considering probes of his companies. Democrats have spent years criticizing his DOGE job cuts and the government contracts and subsidies his companies receive.

Data centers also proved to be a campaign issue well outside Texas. AdImpact counted $173 million in data center ads nationwide, almost entirely negative, with Democratic advertisers behind $140 million of it. Of 41 local data center measures on ballots, at least 10 communities approved bans or restrictions.

For the broad market, the read on Wall Street was friendlier. Jay Woods of Freedom Capital Markets said "there will be gridlock and historically this is good for the market," according to CNBC. Jake Dollarhide of Longbow Asset Management was warier on this sector, telling Reuters a Democratic sweep of Congress "would put pressure on tech and potentially bring regulatory pressure on everything AI." Wedbush's Dan Ives wrote that Musk "needs to find the path forward with Texas now."

How far Hinojosa can take this is unclear. Reuters describes a halt that lasts until legislators strengthen community protections, and none of the coverage we read explains how or when she would impose it. CNBC calls the pledge a moratorium; she has not taken office yet, and SpaceX and Tesla had yet to trade a regular session on the news.
